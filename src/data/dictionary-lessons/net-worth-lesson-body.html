Net Worth

Net Worth is what you truly own once every debt is cleared. You calculate it by adding up everything you own at its current market value and subtracting everything you owe at its outstanding balance. The single number that results — total assets minus total liabilities — is the cleanest snapshot of your financial health, and tracking how it moves over the years tells you far more than any single month of income ever could.

What net worth actually means

Your net worth is a stock, not a flow. Income and spending are flows — they measure money moving through your hands over a period. Net worth is a photograph taken on a single day: it captures the value of everything you have accumulated minus everything you still owe. Two people can earn the same salary yet have wildly different net worth, because one has been saving and investing while the other has been borrowing to fund a lifestyle.

Assets are anything with monetary value you control: bank balances, a fixed deposit, mutual fund units, your provident fund, gold, and property. Liabilities are what you owe: a home loan, car loan, personal loan, or an unpaid credit card bill. Net worth can be positive, zero, or negative — a fresh graduate with an education loan and few savings often starts life in negative territory, and that is perfectly normal.

The formula

The calculation is deliberately simple. You do not need software or an accountant — a single sheet of paper works.

The discipline lies in valuing each side honestly. Count every asset at its current market value, not what you paid for it: a flat bought for ₹40 lakh that would sell for ₹65 lakh today counts as ₹65 lakh. Count every liability at its outstanding balance — the amount still owed to the lender, not the original loan amount. If you took a ₹50 lakh home loan and have repaid ₹12 lakh of principal, the liability is the remaining ₹38 lakh.

How to calculate yours

List every asset in one column with its today value, and every debt in another with its outstanding balance. Total each column and subtract. This mirrors a company balance sheet, where assets equal liabilities plus owner equity — your net worth is simply your personal equity.

Do this once a quarter or once a year on the same date, so the numbers stay comparable.

Why it matters and how to grow it

A rising net worth means you are building genuine wealth; a flat or falling line, despite a good salary, is an early warning. There are only two levers: grow assets or shrink liabilities. Save consistently, invest so your money compounds, keep an emergency fund so you never sell assets in a crisis, and use disciplined budgeting to free up surplus. Paying down high-interest debt lifts net worth just as reliably as earning more. The trend over five and ten years matters far more than any single reading. Worked example
Ananya, a 34-year-old product manager in Pune, wants to know her net worth on 31 March 2026. She lists what she owns and what she owes. Assets: ₹3,20,000 in her savings account, a fixed deposit worth ₹5,00,000, mutual fund units currently valued at ₹8,40,000, an EPF balance of ₹6,10,000, gold jewellery worth ₹4,50,000 at today prices, and a flat that would sell for ₹72,00,000 today. Liabilities: a home loan with ₹41,00,000 still outstanding, a car loan of ₹3,80,000 remaining, and an unpaid credit card bill of ₹70,000. What is her net worth, and what does it tell her?

Step 1 — Total the assets at market value. Ananya adds each asset at what it is worth today, not what she paid. Savings ₹3,20,000 + FD ₹5,00,000 + mutual funds ₹8,40,000 + EPF ₹6,10,000 + gold ₹4,50,000 + flat ₹72,00,000. The total assets come to ₹99,20,000.

Step 2 — Total the liabilities at outstanding balance. She uses the amount still owed on each debt, not the original loan sizes. Home loan ₹41,00,000 + car loan ₹3,80,000 + credit card ₹70,000. The total liabilities come to ₹45,50,000.

ItemAmount (₹)
Total assets99,20,000
Total liabilities45,50,000
Net worth53,70,000

Step 3 — Subtract. Net worth = ₹99,20,000 − ₹45,50,000 = ₹53,70,000. Ananya has a positive net worth of about ₹53.7 lakh.

Step 4 — Read the mix. Her flat makes up most of her assets, so a large share of her wealth is illiquid — she cannot spend it easily. Her liquid and investment assets, excluding the home, are ₹27,20,000, comfortably above her non-home debts. The ₹70,000 credit card balance is her most expensive liability, so clearing it first would raise net worth at the least cost.

So what? One reading is only a starting point. If Ananya repeats this calculation next 31 March and the number has climbed — say to ₹62 lakh through loan repayment, SIP contributions, and market growth — she knows she is genuinely getting wealthier. If it stalls despite a raise, lifestyle creep is eating her surplus. The trend, tracked on the same date each year, is the real scoreboard.

Check yourself
Q
Rahul owns assets worth ₹18,00,000 and owes ₹22,00,000 across an education loan and a personal loan. What is his net worth, and should he panic?
A: Net worth = ₹18,00,000 − ₹22,00,000 = −₹4,00,000. It is negative, which is common early in life. He should not panic — he should focus on repaying debt and building assets so the trend turns upward over time.

Key takeaways

  • Net worth equals total assets minus total liabilities — the truest single measure of financial health.
  • Value assets at current market value and liabilities at their outstanding balance.
  • Net worth can be negative, especially early in life, and that is normal.
  • Grow it by increasing assets, cutting high-interest debt, and investing so money compounds.
  • The trend over years matters far more than any single reading.

Frequently asked questions

Does my salary count in net worth?

No. Salary is income, a flow of money over time. Net worth is a snapshot of what you own minus what you owe on a given day. Salary affects net worth only through what you save and invest from it.

Should I include my house in net worth?

Yes, at its current market value. But remember it is illiquid — you cannot spend a house easily — so it is worth tracking your net worth both with and without the home you live in.

Can net worth be negative?

Yes. If your debts exceed your assets, net worth is negative. This is common for young people with education or home loans. The goal is a rising trend that eventually crosses into positive territory.

How often should I calculate it?

Once a quarter or once a year is enough for most people. Use the same date each time so the readings are comparable and the trend is meaningful.

What is a good net worth in India?

There is no universal number — it depends on age, income, and city. Rather than compare with others, compare your net worth today against your own figure a year ago. Upward movement is the goal.

How is net worth different from a balance sheet?

A company balance sheet lists assets, liabilities, and equity. Your net worth is the personal version of equity — assets minus liabilities. The logic is identical, just applied to a household instead of a business.
