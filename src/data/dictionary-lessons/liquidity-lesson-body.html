Liquidity

Liquidity is how quickly and how cheaply an asset can be turned into cash without knocking its own price down. A liquid asset has buyers waiting near the last traded price, so selling in a hurry costs almost nothing extra, while an illiquid one makes you choose between waiting and accepting less. For an Indian household the ladder runs from a savings balance through liquid funds and listed shares down to property. Those rungs matter most on the day an emergency arrives.

Two liquidities that fail at the same moment

Market liquidity belongs to the asset: it asks whether a willing buyer exists right now, near the price on the screen, for the quantity you hold. Funding liquidity belongs to you, and asks whether you can meet a payment falling due this week out of cash you can reach. That is a different question from whether you are wealthy.

The two collapse together, which is the uncomfortable part. A job loss or a hospital admission arrives precisely when you would rather not sell. That is why an emergency fund sits in boring instruments: its job is to be reachable on a Tuesday afternoon.

The spread and the depth do the measuring

Any share on the NSE order book carries two prices, not one: the highest a buyer will pay and the lowest a seller will accept. The gap between them is the toll for immediacy, and the cleanest single measure of liquidity there is.

P with the subscript ask is the best price a seller is offering, P with the subscript bid the best a buyer is bidding, the denominator the midpoint of the two, and multiplying by 100 states the result as a percentage of price. A stock quoted at 481.90 and 482.00 has a spread near 0.02 per cent; one quoted at 96.20 and 97.55 has a spread near 1.4 per cent.

Spread covers only the first slice of shares; depth covers how many sit behind that quote before the price moves against you. NSE publishes an impact cost measure for exactly that, and no stock enters the Nifty 50 unless its impact cost stays at or below 0.50 per cent across most observations over six months. Liquidity is a screening criterion, not a bonus.

The two assets Indian families hold most of, property and gold, sit at the bottom. That is no argument against owning them, only for holding something near the top too.

Why illiquid assets must promise more

Nobody accepts a lock-in for free. Take two investments with identical risk and expected return, where one can be sold this afternoon and the other cannot be touched for five years: every rupee moves to the first. The second must offer something extra to attract capital, and that extra expected return is the liquidity premium.

It sits inside the yields on lightly traded corporate bonds and the discount on an under-construction flat. The premium is real compensation. The mistake is collecting it with money you need before the lock-in ends, because then volatility and illiquidity combine and you sell on somebody else's terms.

Worked example
Aarti Deshpande, a schoolteacher in Nashik, must deposit ₹6,00,000 at a hospital within four working days. On paper she is worth well over ₹60,00,000. What can she actually raise, and what does the hurry cost her?

Step 1, what the screen says she owns. Her savings balance is ₹90,000, her liquid fund units are worth ₹1,50,000, her demat account shows a mid-cap holding valued at ₹2,40,000, and her flat is worth roughly ₹55,00,000. Added up, the number is comfortable. Added up on a four-day clock, it is not the number that counts.

Step 2, the top of the ladder. The savings balance transfers instantly and in full. The liquid fund is redeemed the same evening and, under the payout norms for such schemes, the money reaches her bank the next business day. Together that is ₹2,40,000 with no loss of value.

Step 3, the shares. The mid-cap is quoted at 96.20 bid and 97.55 offered, a spread of about 1.4 per cent, and the depth behind the best bid is thin. Selling her whole lot walks her down two or three price levels, so the weighted average lands roughly 1.8 per cent below the midpoint on her screen. She banks about ₹2,35,700, and only after settlement.

Step 4, the flat. Four days is not a property timeline. A genuine buyer takes weeks to find and longer to pay, and any offer inside that window carries a distress discount near a tenth of the value. Her largest asset contributes nothing here.

Step 5, closing the gap. She still needs ₹1,24,300 and has run out of liquid assets, so she borrows against an illiquid one: a gold loan sanctioned in a day or two against a pledge. That is the ladder charging her interest for a rung she did not build.

So what? Net worth is not spending power. Aarti was never poor, she was illiquid, and the gap cost her a loss on the shares plus interest on a loan taken only because the calendar was short. Four or five months of expenses parked in a savings account or a liquid fund would have reduced the whole episode to a bank transfer.

Check yourself
Q
Two shares are both quoted at ₹500. One has a spread of 5 paise, the other a spread of ₹4. What does that tell you before you look at anything else?
A: The second is far less liquid, so the round trip costs roughly 0.8 per cent of your money against 0.01 per cent for the first. A wide quote usually sits on a thin book, so size moves the price too.

Key takeaways

  • Liquidity measures speed and price impact together, never speed alone.
  • Market liquidity belongs to the asset, funding liquidity to your household, and both dry up together.
  • Spread plus depth is the working measure, which is why NSE screens Nifty 50 candidates on impact cost.
  • Indian households hold most of their wealth in property and gold, the least liquid rungs.
  • Illiquid assets must offer a higher expected return, a premium safe to collect only with money you will not need soon.

Frequently asked questions

Is a fixed deposit a liquid asset?

Reasonably so, since most deposits can be broken the same day. What you give up is interest, because banks pay the rate applicable to the period the deposit actually ran, usually with a penalty. Tax-saving deposits with a statutory lock-in are the exception.

Are liquid funds risk free?

No. They hold short-maturity debt, so the value moves little day to day, but a credit event in an underlying holding can still hurt. They are chosen for ease of exit, not for a guarantee.

How much of my money should sit in liquid form?

A common working rule is three to six months of essential household expenses, stretched further if your income is irregular. Sizing it against expenses rather than total wealth matters more than the figure itself.

Does high trading volume always mean good liquidity?

Not by itself. Volume can sit in a few minutes or a handful of accounts, leaving the book empty when you want to trade. The spread and the quantity resting behind the best bid and offer describe what you face.

What is the difference between liquidity and solvency?

Solvency asks whether your assets exceed your liabilities. Liquidity asks whether you can pay what falls due this month. Aarti above was solvent and illiquid, and businesses fail on the second count more often.

Do gold and property count as an emergency fund?

Property does not, since its sale timeline runs into months. Gold is a middle case, because a loan against jewellery is sanctioned quickly even though selling takes time.
