EMI

Equated Monthly Instalment, the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan, covering both interest and part of the principal, until the loan is fully paid.

What is an EMI?

An EMI, or Equated Monthly Instalment, is the fixed monthly payment you make to repay a loan such as a home, car, or personal loan. Each instalment is the same size, which makes budgeting predictable across the loan's tenure.

Every EMI has two parts: interest on the outstanding balance, and repayment of principal. Early in the loan the interest part is large and the principal part small; over time this flips, so later EMIs pay down mostly principal. This is called a reducing-balance schedule.

How EMI is calculated

The EMI depends on three things: the loan amount, the interest rate, and the tenure.

P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12 and by 100), and n is the number of monthly instalments.

Example

Worked example

A ₹10,00,000 home loan at 9% per year for 20 years (240 months). Monthly rate r = 0.09/12 = 0.0075.

What is the EMI?
Solution. (1.0075)240 ≈ 6.009. EMI = 10,00,000 × 0.0075 × 6.009 / (6.009 − 1) = about ₹8,997 per month. Over 240 months you repay about ₹21.6 lakh, so roughly ₹11.6 lakh is interest.
Check yourself
Does a longer tenure make a loan cheaper?
No. A longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI but increases the total interest you pay, because you owe the balance for longer. A shorter tenure means a higher EMI but less total interest.

Key takeaways

  • An EMI is a fixed monthly payment of interest plus principal.
  • Early EMIs are mostly interest; later EMIs are mostly principal.
  • A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises total interest paid.
  • Prepaying reduces the outstanding principal and saves future interest.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between fixed and floating rate EMIs?

A fixed-rate loan keeps the same interest rate and EMI throughout. A floating-rate loan's rate moves with the market, so the EMI or the tenure changes when rates change.

Does prepayment reduce my EMI or my tenure?

Usually you can choose. Most lenders let you either keep the EMI and shorten the tenure (saves the most interest) or reduce the EMI and keep the tenure. Reducing tenure generally saves more.

Why is most of my early EMI going to interest?

Because interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is highest at the start. As you repay principal, the balance falls, so a larger share of each later EMI goes to principal.
