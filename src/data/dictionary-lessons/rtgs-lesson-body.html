RTGS

RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement) is the high-value payment rail on which the Reserve Bank of India settles every instruction individually and continuously, rather than bundling it with others. Settlement takes place on the books of the RBI itself, so a completed RTGS transfer is final and cannot be recalled. A customer must remit at least ₹2,00,000 through it, and the RBI sets no upper ceiling, which is why property purchases, business settlements and inter-corporate payments run on this rail. RTGS has been open 24 hours a day on every day of the year since 14 December 2020.

Gross settlement, and why finality is the point

Each instruction settles on its own, one at a time, against the sending bank's balance with the RBI. Nothing waits for a cut-off and nothing is cancelled against other banks, which is what the word gross means here. So the sending bank must hold enough liquidity at the central bank at that instant to cover the full amount, not a netted residue.

Irrevocability is the feature people actually buy. Once the RBI has debited one bank and credited another, no reversal is possible without the beneficiary agreeing to send the money back, which is the comfort a seller wants before parting with a property document. The beneficiary bank must credit the customer within thirty minutes of receiving the message, and where the credit cannot be applied the funds go back within one hour of receipt or by the end of the RTGS business day, whichever is earlier.

A floor of two lakh and no ceiling above it

The ₹2,00,000 minimum is deliberate: RTGS consumes settlement capacity for each transaction, so small payments are pushed towards NEFT, IMPS and UPI, which economise by netting. Above the floor the RBI applies no cap, though your bank may limit what a net banking session sends in a day.

On pricing, the RBI waived its own processing charge from 1 July 2019 and caps what banks may recover from customers. Inward RTGS is free. Outward transfers between ₹2,00,000 and ₹5,00,000 may not be charged more than ₹25 and those above ₹5,00,000 no more than ₹50, in both cases before tax. Treat these as ceilings that the RBI can revise, and read your bank's current schedule.

What certainty costs per rupee moved

Because the charge is a flat rupee cap rather than a percentage, the real cost of RTGS falls away as the amount rises.

Choosing a rail by the size of the payment

Most people meet RTGS once or twice in a lifetime, usually on the day a home loan is disbursed or a sale deed registered. The choice is almost entirely a question of value.

Worked example
Rakesh Iyer is buying a flat in Bopal, Ahmedabad. At the sub-registrar's office on a Thursday morning the builder will not execute the sale deed until ₹48,00,000 has actually landed in his account. Rakesh has the money in his current account and roughly forty minutes before the appointment. Which rail does he use, what does it cost, and when is the builder satisfied?

Step 1, why the other rails are ruled out. IMPS cannot carry ₹48,00,000 in one go, because the RBI ceiling on it is ₹5,00,000 and his bank sets a lower limit still, so he would be firing ten or more transfers while a queue forms behind him. NEFT would carry the amount, but the credit depends on the next batch settling and then a two-hour window, and no builder signs on a promise. RTGS settles his single instruction on the RBI's own books and, once settled, it cannot be pulled back.

Step 2, what certainty costs him. The transfer is above ₹5,00,000, so his bank may not charge more than ₹50 before tax. Adding GST at 18 percent gives ₹59. Applying the cost formula, 59 divided by 48,00,000 and multiplied by 100 is 0.00123 percent, about 0.12 basis points of the money moved. The builder pays nothing at all to receive it.

Step 3, the clock on the day. Rakesh initiates at 11:05 through his branch. Settlement across the RBI's books happens as the instruction is processed, and the builder's bank is then obliged to credit him within thirty minutes of receiving the message, so by roughly 11:35. Had the account details been wrong, the money would have come back within an hour rather than sitting in limbo.

Step 4, the trap he avoided. His net banking screen caps outward RTGS for the day at a level well short of ₹48,00,000, a limit his bank sets rather than the RBI. Doing it at the counter with identification and a signed request form sidesteps that cap entirely, which is why large property payments are still made at a branch.

Check yourself
Q
Why does the RBI insist on a minimum of ₹2,00,000 for a customer RTGS transfer?
A: Every RTGS instruction is settled individually and uses real settlement capacity and bank liquidity. Steering smaller payments to the netting systems keeps the gross settlement rail free for the high-value transfers that genuinely need immediate finality.

Key takeaways

  • RTGS settles each instruction separately and continuously on the RBI's books, with no batching and no netting.
  • A settled RTGS transfer is final and irrevocable, which is why it is the default for property and other high-value payments.
  • The customer minimum is ₹2,00,000 and the RBI sets no maximum, although banks cap what individual channels may send.
  • Inward RTGS is free; outward charges are capped at ₹25 up to ₹5,00,000 and ₹50 above it, before tax.
  • The beneficiary must be credited within thirty minutes of the message, and failed transfers returned within an hour.

Frequently asked questions

Can I send less than ₹2,00,000 by RTGS?

No. Customer transactions below that floor are rejected, and the payment should go through NEFT, IMPS or UPI instead. The floor applies to customer remittances, not to settlement between banks themselves.

Is RTGS available at night and on holidays?

Yes, RTGS has run 24 hours a day on all days since 14 December 2020. Banks may still keep shorter counter hours or run maintenance windows, so availability of the system is not the same as availability of your bank.

Can an RTGS transfer be reversed?

Not once it has settled. If the beneficiary details were wrong and the credit cannot be applied, the money returns automatically. If it reached the wrong but valid account, recovery depends on that account holder agreeing to return it, so verify details before you confirm.

What is the maximum RTGS amount?

The RBI prescribes none. Practical ceilings come from your bank and vary by channel, with net banking usually capped well below what a branch will process.

What details do I need to send an RTGS?

The amount, your account number, and the beneficiary name, account number, account type, branch and IFSC. Eligible entities must also quote a Legal Entity Identifier on qualifying transactions.

Is RTGS faster than IMPS or UPI?

In wall-clock terms, no. IMPS and UPI credit a beneficiary in seconds, while an RTGS credit is due within thirty minutes. What RTGS offers instead is unlimited value and settlement that is final on the central bank's books.
