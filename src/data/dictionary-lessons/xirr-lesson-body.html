FINYAAR DICTIONARY · MUTUAL FUNDS AND SIP

XIRR

XIRR is the annualised rate of return on a series of cash flows that happen on irregular dates, which is what almost every real Indian portfolio looks like. It finds the one yearly rate at which every rupee you paid in, grown from its own date, adds up exactly to what you took out or still hold. Fund platforms, registrars and consolidated account statements report SIP returns this way because instalments arrive monthly and redemptions arrive whenever life demands. The letter X stands for the dates: unlike a plain internal rate of return, XIRR reads the actual calendar.

A rupee in April is not a rupee in October

Suppose four instalments of ₹10,000 go into a fund on the first of four consecutive months and the whole holding is sold later. The first instalment worked for six months, the last for three. Any measure treating the ₹40,000 as one block invested on day one describes an investment nobody made.

This is the time value of money arriving through the back door of a SIP. Each instalment buys units at that day's NAV and compounds from that day, so an honest return figure carries a date beside every amount.

The equation a spreadsheet solves for you

XIRR is defined by the rate that makes a portfolio break even when every flow is pulled back to a common date.

C with subscript i is the i-th cash flow, negative when money leaves your bank and positive when it comes back. The letter d with subscript i is the date of that flow and d with subscript one is the date of the first, so the difference is a count of days, divided by 365 to turn it into a fraction of a year. The letter r is the annual rate being solved for, and the denominator discounts each flow back to the first date.

In words, discounting asks what a future rupee is worth today at rate r. XIRR searches for the rate at which everything you paid in exactly cancels everything you took out. No formula isolates r, so the software iterates until the sum is close enough to zero.

Getting the signs right in a spreadsheet

Two columns do the whole job in any mutual fund tracker, one of dates and one of amounts, followed by a single XIRR call on the amount range and the date range. Whatever goes wrong goes wrong in the sign column.

Two limits worth knowing

XIRR annualises, which makes short periods look dramatic. A holding up 4 per cent in seven weeks reports an XIRR above 30 per cent, saying only what would happen if that pace held for a year. Treat anything under a full year as a curiosity.

It also expects the flows to change sign at least once, and an unusual pattern can admit more than one valid answer, in which case the spreadsheet returns whichever it reaches first. For a plain purchase and sale, XIRR and CAGR agree exactly, so the extra machinery earns its keep only when the dates are messy.

Worked example
Farhan Qureshi, a hotel manager in Hyderabad, ran a short SIP of ₹10,000 on the first of April, May, June and July 2025, then redeemed the entire holding on 1 October 2025 for ₹43,000. He tells a colleague he made 7.5 per cent. What was his actual annualised return?

Step 1, the flows and their dates. Four debits of ₹10,000 leave his account on 1 April, 1 May, 1 June and 1 July, each entered as negative. One credit of ₹43,000 arrives on 1 October, entered as positive. Total paid in is ₹40,000, so the absolute gain of ₹3,000 is indeed 7.5 per cent of what he invested.

Step 2, how long each rupee worked. Counting calendar days to 1 October gives 183, 153, 122 and 92 respectively. The average is 137.5 days, well under five months, so 7.5 per cent was earned in a good deal less than half a year. That single fact is what the 7.5 per cent figure leaves out.

Step 3, solving for the rate. The spreadsheet searches for the annual rate at which the four instalments, each grown from its own date, total ₹43,000 on 1 October. It settles at 21.11 per cent. Growing each instalment at that rate confirms it.

Step 4, why the naive method is so far off. Pretend instead that the full ₹40,000 went in on 1 April. That is 7.5 per cent over 183 days, and annualising it with the CAGR formula gives about 15.52 per cent. The gap of more than five and a half percentage points comes entirely from the pretence, because three quarters of his money was not in the market on 1 April at all.

So what? Farhan earned 21.11 per cent a year on the money he actually had working, not 7.5 per cent and not 15.5 per cent. Since his money went in on four different dates, the only defensible figure is the one that reads those dates. Build the two-column table from your consolidated account statement, remember the closing value as a positive flow if you have not sold, and treat any XIRR on a stub period of a few months as information rather than a promise.

Check yourself
Q
You enter all your SIP instalments as negative amounts with their dates and the function returns an error. You have not redeemed anything. What is missing?
A: A positive flow. Add today's market value of the holding on today's date as a positive amount. XIRR needs at least one inflow and one outflow to have a rate to solve for.

Key takeaways

  • XIRR annualises returns when money goes in and out on irregular dates, which is the normal case.
  • It solves for the rate at which all dated flows, discounted to a common date, sum to zero.
  • Money leaving your bank is negative, money returning is positive, and unsold holdings enter as today's value on today's date.
  • For a single purchase and a single sale, XIRR and CAGR agree exactly.
  • Annualising a period of a few months exaggerates, so judge SIP performance over years.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good XIRR for a SIP in India?

It depends on the asset class and the period. Judge an equity SIP against the scheme's benchmark index and its category over the same dates.

Why is my XIRR higher than the fund's published CAGR?

They measure different things. The fund publishes point-to-point growth for a lump sum, while your XIRR reflects the dates your own instalments went in. Buying through a weak stretch lifts your XIRR above the scheme's CAGR, and the reverse happens too.

Can XIRR be negative?

Yes, whenever what you hold plus what you have withdrawn falls short of what you paid in. Early in an equity SIP a negative XIRR is common and says little, since few instalments have had time to work.

Does XIRR work for a portfolio holding several funds?

It does. Put every purchase and redemption across all schemes into one dated list and add the combined current value as the final positive flow.

Is XIRR the same as IRR?

The idea is the same, but IRR assumes equally spaced flows while XIRR takes explicit dates and a 365-day year. For monthly instalments and an irregular redemption, XIRR is the correct function.

Should XIRR be calculated before or after tax?

Platforms report it before tax. To see what you keep, enter redemption proceeds net of capital gains tax. Confirm applicable rates with the Income Tax Department for your assessment year.
