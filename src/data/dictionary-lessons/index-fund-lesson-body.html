FINYAAR DICTIONARY · MUTUAL FUNDS AND SIP

Index Fund

a mutual fund that passively tracks a market index such as the Nifty 50, aiming to match the index's return at very low cost rather than to beat it.

What is an index fund?

An index fund is a mutual fund that simply copies a market index, such as the Nifty 50 or the Sensex. It holds the same stocks in the same proportions as the index, so its return closely mirrors the index's return. The aim is not to beat the market but to be the market, cheaply.

This is called passive investing. There is no fund manager picking stocks, so costs are low. Because most active funds fail to beat their index consistently after fees, low-cost index funds have become a popular core holding.

How index funds work

When the index changes its constituents or weights, the fund adjusts to match. The small gap between the fund's return and the index's return, caused by costs and cash holdings, is called tracking error; lower is better. The expense ratio of a good index fund is a fraction of that of an active fund, and that saved cost stays in your returns.

Example

Worked example

An active fund and a Nifty 50 index fund both deliver a 12% gross return in a year. The active fund charges 1.8% and the index fund 0.2%.

What do you actually keep?
Solution. Active: 12% − 1.8% = 10.2%. Index: 12% − 0.2% = 11.8%. Same gross return, but the lower-cost index fund leaves 1.6% more in your pocket every year, which compounds into a large gap over time.

Key takeaways

  • An index fund copies an index instead of trying to beat it.
  • Passive management keeps the expense ratio very low.
  • Lower costs and low tracking error mean more of the return stays with you.
  • It is a simple, diversified core holding for long-term investors.

Frequently asked questions

Is an index fund better than an active fund?

Not always, but over long periods most active funds fail to beat their index after fees, so low-cost index funds often win on net returns. Active funds can outperform in specific segments, but consistently picking those in advance is hard.

What is tracking error?

It is the small difference between an index fund's return and the index it follows, caused by fund costs, cash holdings, and timing. A well-run index fund keeps tracking error low.

Can I do a SIP in an index fund?

Yes. Index funds work well with SIPs, combining low cost with rupee cost averaging for disciplined long-term investing.
