What net worth actually means

Net worth is a stock, not a flow. Income and spending measure activity over a period; net worth is a snapshot on one date. Two people with the same income can have very different net worth because their saving, investing, borrowing and past circumstances differ.

Assets include cash, deposits, investments, retirement balances, gold, property and other items with a reasonable resale value. Liabilities include outstanding home, education, vehicle and personal loans, credit-card balances, taxes due and other amounts owed. Net worth can be positive, zero or negative. A negative figure is not automatically a crisis, but its cause, debt cost and trend deserve attention.

The formula

Use a realistic current value for each asset, preferably the amount it could reasonably realise after material selling costs rather than an optimistic asking price. Use the lender’s current outstanding balance for each debt, not the original amount borrowed or the sum of future instalments.

A home can be included as an asset with its loan shown separately as a liability. Because a home, jewellery and vehicles are not readily spendable, many households also track liquid or investable net worth, excluding personal-use assets. That second number helps assess financial flexibility.

How to calculate yours

Choose a valuation date, list every asset and liability, total both columns and subtract. Avoid counting a financed asset net of its loan and then subtracting the loan again: record the asset at full current value and the debt separately. This resembles a personal balance sheet.

Repeat the calculation on the same date each quarter or year and use consistent valuation rules. Frequent estimates of illiquid property can create noise that looks like progress.

How net worth changes

Net worth generally rises when you retain income, investment values grow, an asset appreciates, or a liability is reduced without an equal fall in assets. It falls when spending exceeds income, assets lose value, interest and fees accrue, or new borrowing funds consumption.

One subtle point matters: using ₹1 lakh of existing cash to repay ₹1 lakh of debt reduces assets and liabilities equally, so net worth is unchanged immediately. The benefit comes later through interest avoided and lower financial risk. Budgeting, consistent investing and an emergency fund can support the long-term trend.

Net worth is useful, but not a complete health score. A household can have high net worth concentrated in a home yet little cash, inadequate insurance or unaffordable monthly repayments. Track liquidity, cash flow, debt costs and goal progress alongside it.

Worked example
On 31 March 2026, Ananya lists ₹3,20,000 in savings, a ₹5,00,000 fixed deposit, mutual funds worth ₹8,40,000, an EPF balance of ₹6,10,000, gold with an estimated resale value of ₹4,50,000 and a flat with a reasonable current value of ₹72,00,000. She owes ₹41,00,000 on her home loan, ₹3,80,000 on a personal loan and ₹70,000 on a credit card. What is her net worth, and what else should she notice?

Step 1 — Total the assets. ₹3,20,000 + ₹5,00,000 + ₹8,40,000 + ₹6,10,000 + ₹4,50,000 + ₹72,00,000 = ₹99,20,000.

Step 2 — Total the liabilities. ₹41,00,000 + ₹3,80,000 + ₹70,000 = ₹45,50,000.

Step 3 — Subtract. ₹99,20,000 − ₹45,50,000 = ₹53,70,000.

Step 4 — Read beyond the headline. Her home represents most of her assets, so total net worth overstates immediately available resources. Of the ₹27.2 lakh outside the home, some—such as EPF and gold—may not be instantly accessible or may incur costs.

Step 5 — Understand debt repayment. If Ananya uses ₹70,000 from savings to clear the card, assets become ₹98.5 lakh and liabilities become ₹44.8 lakh. Net worth remains ₹53.7 lakh immediately, but she avoids future card interest and lowers risk.

So what? Her result is a useful baseline, not a verdict. Repeating the calculation with consistent values next year will show whether retained income, investment growth and interest savings are building wealth.

Check yourself
Rahul owns assets worth ₹18,00,000 and owes ₹22,00,000. What is his net worth, and what should he examine before deciding whether the negative figure is serious?
A: His net worth is ₹18,00,000 − ₹22,00,000 = −₹4,00,000. He should examine the interest rates, repayment affordability, liquidity, purpose of the debt and whether his net worth is improving. A negative figure can be common early in life, but expensive or unmanageable debt needs prompt attention.

Key takeaways

  • Net worth equals current assets minus outstanding liabilities on one date.
  • Use realistic values and keep the valuation method consistent.
  • Track liquid or investable net worth separately when much of your wealth is tied up in a home.
  • Paying debt with existing cash does not immediately change net worth, but interest saved can improve it later.
  • Read the trend alongside liquidity, cash flow, debt cost, insurance and financial goals.

Frequently asked questions

Does salary count in net worth?

No. Salary is a flow; only the portion retained as an asset or used to change liabilities affects the snapshot.

Should I include my home?

Usually yes, with its loan separate, while also tracking liquid or investable net worth.

Can net worth be negative?

Yes. The cause, repayment affordability and direction of the trend matter.

How often should I calculate it?

Quarterly or annually, using a consistent date and valuation method.

What is a good net worth in India?

There is no universal figure; compare progress with your goals and overall financial position.

Does repaying debt immediately increase net worth?

Not when existing cash is used, because assets and liabilities fall equally. Future interest saved can improve net worth.
