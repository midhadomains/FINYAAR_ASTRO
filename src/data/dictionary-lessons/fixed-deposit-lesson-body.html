FINYAAR DICTIONARY · BANKING SAVINGS AND DEPOSITS

Fixed Deposit

a deposit where you lock a lump sum with a bank or NBFC for a fixed term at a fixed interest rate, earning guaranteed returns whatever the market does.

What is a fixed deposit?

A fixed deposit (FD) is one of the simplest and safest ways to grow savings in India. You place a lump sum with a bank or NBFC for a chosen period, from a few days to ten years, at an interest rate that is locked in on the day you book it. At maturity you receive a guaranteed, predetermined amount, no matter how markets move.

Because the rate is fixed and bank deposits are insured up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (under DICGC), an FD carries very little risk. The trade-off is lower long-term returns than market-linked options such as equity.

How a fixed deposit works

You choose the amount and tenure, and the bank quotes a rate for that tenure. Interest can be paid out at regular intervals (non-cumulative, good for regular income) or added back and compounded until maturity (cumulative, which grows faster). Senior citizens usually earn a slightly higher rate.

Breaking an FD before maturity earns a lower rate and may attract a small penalty, so an FD suits money you will not need during the chosen period.

For a cumulative FD, A is the maturity amount, P the deposit, r the annual rate, n the compounding frequency, and t the years.

Example

Worked example

You deposit ₹1,00,000 in a cumulative FD at 7% per year, compounded quarterly, for 3 years.

What is the maturity amount?
Solution. A = 1,00,000 × (1 + 0.07/4)12 = 1,00,000 × 1.2314 = about ₹1,23,100. The extra ₹23,100 over three years is fully guaranteed at the rate locked in on day one.

Key takeaways

  • Returns are fixed and guaranteed, with very low risk.
  • Cumulative FDs compound and grow faster; non-cumulative FDs pay regular income.
  • Breaking an FD early lowers your interest and may cost a penalty.
  • Returns usually trail equity over the long run, so FDs suit safety and short horizons.

Frequently asked questions

Is FD interest taxable?

Yes. FD interest is fully taxable as per your income-tax slab, and banks deduct TDS once your interest crosses the annual threshold. You must still report it even if no TDS is deducted.

What is a tax-saving FD?

A 5-year tax-saver FD qualifies for a deduction under Section 80C, up to the 80C limit. In return it has a 5-year lock-in and does not allow premature withdrawal.

Is a fixed deposit safe?

Bank FDs are among the safest options: the rate is fixed and deposits are insured up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank. NBFC or company FDs can offer higher rates but carry more risk.
