IPO

an Initial Public Offering: the first time a private company sells its shares to the public and lists on a stock exchange, raising money and letting anyone become a shareholder.

What is an IPO?

An Initial Public Offering (IPO) is the moment a privately owned company first sells shares to the general public and gets listed on a stock exchange such as the NSE or BSE. Before the IPO, ownership is held by founders, early investors, and insiders; after it, anyone can buy the shares.

Companies go public mainly to raise money for growth or to give early investors a way to sell, and listing also raises the company's profile. For investors, an IPO is a chance to buy into a company as it enters the public market.

How an IPO works

The company, with investment banks, sets a price band and offers shares through a book-building process where investors bid within the band. Buying shares in the IPO is the primary market. Once the shares list and start trading between investors, that is the secondary market. In India you typically apply online using the ASBA or UPI process, and money is blocked in your bank account until shares are allotted.

What to watch out for

An IPO is not a guaranteed profit. Listing gains can happen, but shares can also list below the issue price. Hype and heavy demand do not guarantee long-term value, so it is worth reading the company's prospectus, understanding how it makes money, and judging the valuation rather than chasing the buzz.

Key takeaways

  • An IPO is a company's first sale of shares to the public.
  • Companies use it to raise capital and let early investors exit.
  • You apply in the primary market; later trading happens in the secondary market.
  • Listing gains are not guaranteed; judge the business and valuation.

Frequently asked questions

How do I apply for an IPO in India?

You apply online through your bank or broker using the ASBA or UPI process. The application amount is blocked in your bank account and is debited only if shares are allotted to you.

Is investing in an IPO safe?

An IPO carries real risk. Some list at a premium and some below the issue price, and short-term demand does not guarantee long-term value. Read the prospectus and assess the business before applying.

What is the difference between the primary and secondary market?

The primary market is where the company first issues shares (the IPO). The secondary market is the stock exchange where investors then buy and sell those shares among themselves.
