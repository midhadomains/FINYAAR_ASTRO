Equity Share

Equity Share is a unit of ownership in a company that gives the holder a proportionate claim on the firm's profits and assets, along with voting rights at shareholder meetings. Equity Share returns come from two sources — capital appreciation in the share price and dividends — but both are variable and carry no guarantee.

What is an Equity Share?

An Equity Share (also called an ordinary share or common stock) is the most basic form of ownership in a company. When you buy an equity share, you become a part-owner of the business — entitled to a slice of its profits and a vote in key decisions. Unlike a lender, an equity shareholder is a residual claimant: you get paid only after employees, suppliers, lenders and preference shareholders are satisfied. That is why equity carries the highest risk and, over long horizons, the highest expected reward.

Think of Reliance Industries. Its shares trade on the NSE and BSE, and the company has roughly 676 crore shares outstanding. If you buy 50 of them, you own a tiny fraction of every refinery, telecom tower and retail store Reliance owns. Companies first sell equity shares to the public through an IPO, after which the shares change hands freely on the secondary market — the Sensex and Nifty simply track baskets of such shares.

Example

Worked example

Ravi, a Pune-based investor, buys 100 equity shares of a mid-cap company at ₹500 per share. During the year the company declares a dividend of ₹8 per share. Twelve months later Ravi sells all shares at ₹575. Assume total transaction costs (STT, brokerage, GST) of about ₹80. What is his return, and how does it split between income and price gain?

Step 1 — Total investment. 100 shares × ₹500 = ₹50,000 deployed upfront.

Step 2 — Dividend income. 100 × ₹8 = ₹800. As a percentage of cost, the Dividend Yield is 8 / 500 = 1.6%.

Step 3 — Capital gain. Sale value = 100 × ₹575 = ₹57,500. Gain = ₹57,500 − ₹50,000 = ₹7,500, i.e. 75 / 500 = 15% price appreciation.

Step 4 — Total gross return. Using the formula, (7,500 + 800) / 50,000 × 100 = 8,300 / 50,000 × 100 = 16.6%.

Step 5 — Net of costs. After ₹80 of charges, net gain = ₹8,220, so net return ≈ 16.4%.

So what? Ravi's 16.6% comfortably beat the ~7% a Fixed Deposit would have paid, and it stayed well ahead of Inflation (the RBI targets 4% CPI within a 2–6% band). But notice the make-up: 90% of his profit came from an uncertain price rise, only 10% from dividends. Had the share fallen to ₹430 instead, his ₹800 dividend would not have saved him — he would have booked a −12.4% loss. That asymmetry is the essence of equity: high potential reward, no floor. Note also that since Ravi held for exactly a year, gains just over 12 months qualify as long-term capital gains, taxed at 12.5% above the ₹1.25 lakh annual exemption under current rules.

Check yourself
A company has 20 crore equity shares outstanding, each trading at ₹250, with an EPS of ₹10. What are its market capitalization and P/E ratio?

Market Cap = ₹250 × 20 crore = ₹5,000 crore (a small-cap by SEBI's classification). P/E = 250 / 10 = 25, meaning investors pay ₹25 for every ₹1 of annual earnings.

Key takeaways

  • An equity share is a unit of ownership, giving voting rights and a residual claim on profits and assets.
  • Returns come from capital appreciation plus dividends; neither is guaranteed.
  • Equity shareholders are paid last, which is why equity is the riskiest but historically highest-returning asset class.
  • Key metrics: EPS, P/E ratio, dividend yield, beta and market capitalization.
  • Most Indians access equity efficiently through mutual funds, index funds and SIPs for built-in diversification.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an equity share and a preference share?

Equity shares carry voting rights and variable dividends, and rank last for payouts. Preference shares usually have no vote but receive a fixed dividend and rank ahead of equity if the company winds up. Equity offers higher upside; preference offers steadier income.

How do I buy equity shares in India?

Open a demat and trading account with a SEBI-registered broker, complete KYC, add funds, and place a buy order on the NSE or BSE. Shares are credited to your demat account by T+1. You can also gain exposure through an IPO or an equity mutual fund.

Are equity shares safe?

They carry no capital protection — prices can and do fall, and a company can go bankrupt, wiping out equity holders. Over long horizons of 7–10 years or more, diversified equity has historically beaten inflation and fixed deposits, but short-term losses are common. Never invest money you may need within a few years.

How are equity share gains taxed in India?

Listed shares held over 12 months attract long-term capital gains tax at 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year. Shares held 12 months or less attract short-term capital gains at 20%. Dividends are taxed at your income-tax slab rate.

Do equity shares always pay dividends?

No. Dividends are discretionary — the board decides each year. Many high-growth companies pay little or nothing, reinvesting profits to expand. In such cases your entire return depends on the share price rising.

What voting rights do equity shareholders have?

Each equity share typically carries one vote on resolutions such as electing directors, approving auditors and major decisions, exercised at the AGM or via e-voting. Small retail holders have limited practical influence, but the right is real and legally protected.
