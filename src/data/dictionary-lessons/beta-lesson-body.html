Beta

a measure of how much a stock or portfolio tends to move relative to the overall market, used to gauge its market-related (systematic) risk.

What is beta?

Beta tells you how sensitive an investment is to movements in the broader market. The market itself, represented by an index such as the Nifty 50 or S&P 500, has a beta of exactly 1. A stock's beta compares its swings to that benchmark.

A beta above 1 means the stock tends to move more than the market: it rises more in rallies and falls more in downturns. A beta below 1 means it moves less than the market and is generally steadier. A beta near 0 means little relationship with the market, and a negative beta means it tends to move opposite to the market.

How to read beta values

Beta = 1.5: if the market rises 10%, the stock tends to rise about 15%, and fall harder when the market drops. Beta = 0.6: the stock typically moves only 6% for a 10% market move, making it more defensive. High-beta names are often growth or cyclical stocks; low-beta names are often stable sectors like utilities or consumer staples.

In words, beta is how the stock moves with the market (their covariance), scaled by how much the market itself moves (its variance).

Example

Worked example

A stock has a beta of 1.3. The market falls 8% in a week.

Roughly how much would you expect the stock to move?
Solution. Expected move ≈ 1.3 × (−8%) = −10.4%. The higher beta amplified the market's fall. In a rising market the same beta would amplify the gain, which is the trade-off of holding higher-beta stocks.

Key takeaways

  • Beta measures market risk, not total risk or the quality of a company.
  • Beta of 1 moves with the market; above 1 is more volatile, below 1 is more defensive.
  • It is central to CAPM, which uses beta to estimate the return a stock should offer.
  • Beta is backward-looking and can change over time, so treat it as a guide, not a guarantee.

Frequently asked questions

What does a beta of 1.2 mean?

It means the stock has tended to move about 20% more than the market. If the market rises or falls 10%, a beta of 1.2 suggests the stock moves roughly 12% in the same direction, on average.

Is a high or low beta better?

Neither is universally better. High-beta stocks offer bigger gains in rising markets but bigger losses in falling ones, suiting aggressive investors. Low-beta stocks are steadier and suit those wanting less volatility.

Can beta be negative?

Yes, though it is rare. A negative beta means the asset tends to move opposite to the market. Gold and certain hedging assets sometimes show this behaviour, which can make them useful for reducing portfolio risk.
