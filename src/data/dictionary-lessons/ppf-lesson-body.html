PPF (Public Provident Fund)

PPF (Public Provident Fund) is a long-term, government-backed small-savings scheme in India that accepts between ₹500 and ₹1.5 lakh in eligible deposits each financial year and earns a government-notified rate of compound interest. Under current tax rules, eligible contributions can qualify within the combined Section 80C limit under the old regime, while interest and qualifying maturity proceeds are exempt.

What a PPF account is

A PPF account is a savings scheme created by the Government of India and offered through designated post offices and banks. The government-backed framework makes it one of the lowest-credit-risk investments available to individuals. A resident individual can open one account in their own name, and a guardian can open an account on behalf of a minor. It is designed for patient, long-term wealth building rather than short-term liquidity, so it can suit retirement savings, a child’s future or another distant goal. Unlike market-linked products, its return does not move directly with the stock market, which is why some savers pair PPF with a growth product such as an index fund.

How it works: tenure, limits, interest and tax

The account matures after fifteen complete financial years from the end of the financial year in which it was opened. You must deposit at least ₹500 in a financial year to keep the account active, and eligible aggregate deposits cannot exceed ₹1.5 lakh a year under the scheme rules. Interest is set by the government and notified periodically; it is 7.1% for the July–September 2026 quarter, but it can change. Interest is calculated on the lowest balance between the close of the fifth day and the end of each month and credited annually. Depositing before or on 5 April generally allows an annual lump sum to earn interest for the full month. Withdrawals, loans and extension

PPF is deliberately illiquid, but it is not completely inaccessible. A loan can be available from the third financial year through the sixth under the balance-based limits. Partial withdrawals are permitted after five years have expired from the end of the opening year, commonly described as being available from the seventh financial year, subject to the prescribed balance limit and one-withdrawal-per-year rule. At maturity, an account holder can close the account, retain it without further deposits while it continues to earn the prevailing rate, or extend it in five-year blocks with deposits. Premature closure is limited to specified circumstances and can reduce the credited interest.

Where a PPF account fits

PPF can act as a low-credit-risk, tax-efficient anchor in a retirement and planning portfolio. It sits alongside the NPS for retirement and can complement a bank fixed deposit, whose interest is generally taxable. PPF does not guarantee an inflation-beating return: whether it preserves purchasing power depends on future notified rates and inflation. Its main trade-off is straightforward—government-backed stability and favourable current tax treatment in exchange for limited liquidity.

Key takeaways

  • A PPF account is a government-backed, long-term small-savings account for individuals.
  • Eligible aggregate deposits range from ₹500 to ₹1.5 lakh per financial year under the scheme rules.
  • The government-notified rate is 7.1% for July–September 2026 and can change in later quarters.
  • Under current rules, eligible contributions can use Section 80C under the old regime, while interest and qualifying maturity proceeds are exempt.
  • Loans, partial withdrawals, premature closure and five-year extensions are available only under their respective conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Can I have more than one PPF account?

An individual may maintain one PPF account in their own name. A guardian may open one for a minor, but the ₹1.5 lakh annual ceiling is inclusive of deposits made in the individual’s own account and in accounts opened on behalf of minors.

What happens if I miss a deposit in a year?

The account becomes discontinued if the ₹500 minimum is not deposited. It can generally be revived by paying the prescribed fee and arrears for each default year. A discontinued account continues to earn applicable interest, but some facilities remain restricted until revival.

Is the PPF interest rate fixed for 15 years?

No. The government notifies the rate periodically, currently by quarter. It can change during the account’s life, so projections using today’s rate are illustrative.

Can I withdraw all my money before maturity?

Premature closure is allowed only after the prescribed period and for specified grounds, subject to documents and an interest-rate reduction. Otherwise, partial withdrawals follow the scheme rules.

How is PPF different from a fixed deposit?

A fixed deposit offers selectable tenures and generally pays taxable interest. PPF has a longer maturity period, a government-notified variable rate and tax-exempt interest and qualifying maturity proceeds under current rules.

Does the ₹1.5 lakh limit include my other Section 80C investments?

The scheme limits aggregate eligible PPF deposits to ₹1.5 lakh a year. Separately, the combined deduction across eligible Sections 80C, 80CCC and 80CCD(1) items is capped at ₹1.5 lakh under the old regime.
