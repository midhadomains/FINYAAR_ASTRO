Credit Score (CIBIL)

a three-digit number, in India usually between 300 and 900, that summarises how reliably you repay borrowed money; lenders use it to decide whether to lend to you and at what rate.

What is a credit score?

A credit score is a three-digit number that captures your track record with borrowed money. In India it usually ranges from 300 to 900, and a higher score means you look less risky to lenders. A score of around 750 or above is generally considered good.

CIBIL is the best-known of India's credit bureaus; Experian, Equifax, and CRIF High Mark also produce scores. Each builds your score from the credit history reported by banks and lenders: your loans, credit cards, and how you have repaid them.

What affects your score

Repayment history matters most: paying EMIs and card bills in full and on time. Credit utilisation (how much of your card limit you use) should stay low, ideally well under a third. Length of history, a healthy mix of secured and unsecured credit, and the number of recent hard enquiries (loan or card applications) also count. Many applications in a short time can pull the score down.

Why it matters and how to improve it

A high score gets loans approved faster and often at lower interest rates, saving real money over a loan's life. A low score can mean rejection or a higher rate. To improve it: never miss a due date, keep card usage low, avoid applying for many loans at once, keep old accounts open, and check your report for errors.

Key takeaways

  • A credit score (300 to 900 in India) signals how reliably you repay.
  • On-time repayment and low credit-card utilisation matter most.
  • A score around 750+ is generally treated as good.
  • A better score means easier approvals and lower interest rates.

Frequently asked questions

How can I check my credit score for free?

Each bureau (CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, CRIF) must provide one free full credit report a year, and many banks and apps show your score for free. Checking your own score is a soft enquiry and does not lower it.

Does checking my score hurt it?

No. Checking your own score is a soft enquiry with no impact. Only hard enquiries, when a lender checks your report because you applied for credit, can slightly lower the score.

How long does it take to improve a credit score?

There is no instant fix. Consistent on-time payments and low utilisation usually show improvement over several months, and it can take longer to recover from missed payments or a default.
