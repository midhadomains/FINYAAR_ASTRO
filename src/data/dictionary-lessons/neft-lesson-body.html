NEFT

NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer) is the Reserve Bank of India's nationwide retail payment system for moving money between bank accounts in half-hourly settlement batches. It works on deferred net settlement, so instructions collected in each window are netted across banks and settled together rather than one payment at a time. The system has run around the clock on all 365 days of the year since December 2019, and since 1 January 2020 banks do not charge savings account holders for NEFT started online. The RBI fixes neither a minimum nor a maximum amount, though your bank sets its own channel limits.

How one half-hourly batch moves your money

Your bank debits your account the moment you confirm, turns the instruction into a structured message, and sends it to the NEFT centre run by the RBI. Nothing has reached the beneficiary yet. The message waits for the next cut-off, joins every other instruction in that window, and settles as part of a batch. Forty-eight batches run through the day, the first at 00:30 hours and the last at midnight, on Sundays and holidays too.

What follows is enforceable. The destination bank must credit the beneficiary within two hours of the batch settling, or return the money within two hours of the batch completing where the credit fails, usually because the account is closed or the name mismatches. Miss either deadline and the bank owes penal interest at the prevailing repo rate plus two percentage points, credited without any complaint from you.

What a NEFT transfer costs

For most retail users the answer is nothing. The RBI dropped its own processing charge in July 2019 and then told banks to stop charging savings account holders for online transfers from 1 January 2020, so a net banking or mobile NEFT from an ordinary savings account is free at any value. Inward NEFT is free too.

Fees survive at the branch counter and on non-savings accounts, where the RBI caps recovery in slabs rising from a couple of rupees to a few tens of rupees, plus GST. Those are ceilings rather than prices, so check your bank's current tariff sheet.

When NEFT is the sensible rail

Batching suits anything planned in advance: salaries, vendor bills, rent, loan instalments, premiums. Each transfer carries a payer name and a remarks field and lands with a clean reference in both statements, which matters to a business pushing hundreds of payments a month. It is the wrong tool when a payment must be final within minutes, which is the job of RTGS, or when the payee is watching the screen for a credit, which is what IMPS and UPI are for.

Worked example
Anjali Deshmukh rents a flat in Baner, Pune, and owes her landlord ₹32,000 on the fifth of every month. She works late on the fourth and opens net banking at 21:12, long after her branch has shut, and her landlord banks elsewhere. Will the rent count as paid on time, and what does a year of these transfers cost her?

Step 1, what it costs to move the money. This is a NEFT from a savings account started online, so the charge is nil. Twelve payments carry ₹3,84,000 across the year for free, and her landlord pays nothing to receive them either, because inward NEFT may not be charged. Had she walked into a branch with a paper form each month, the counter slab for amounts between ₹10,000 and ₹1,00,000 would apply, a few rupees plus GST twelve times over, turning a free service into a small and pointless annual fee.

Step 2, when the money actually settles. Her bank debits ₹32,000 at 21:12 and queues the message. The 21:30 cut-off sweeps it into a batch, nets it against everything else in that window, and settles it between the two banks. Her landlord's bank must then credit him within two hours, so by 23:30 at the outside, on a date that is still the fourth. Rent due on the fifth is comfortably met, and both statements will show the same unique transaction reference.

Step 3, what happens if it breaks. Suppose she mistyped one digit of the account number. The destination bank cannot apply the credit and must return ₹32,000 within two hours of the batch completing. Should her account not be re-credited by then, penal interest at the repo rate plus two percentage points runs in her favour for the period of delay, without her having to ask.

So what? A payment due on a date rather than at a moment does not need an instant rail. Anjali gets the rent there on time, for nothing, at ten at night on a day her branch was shut, and the two-hour rule hands her a firm deadline she can hold her bank to when something goes wrong.

Check yourself
Q
A NEFT batch settles at 14:00 and the beneficiary bank cannot credit the account. By when must the money come back?
A: Within two hours of that batch completing, so by roughly 16:00. If your account is re-credited later, the bank owes penal interest at the repo rate plus two percentage points for the delay.

Key takeaways

  • NEFT settles in forty-eight half-hourly batches every day of the year, netting obligations between banks rather than settling each payment.
  • You are debited at once, but the payee is credited within two hours of the batch settling, not of your click.
  • Online NEFT from a savings account has carried no bank charge since 1 January 2020, and inward NEFT is free everywhere.
  • The RBI sets no minimum and no maximum; any ceiling you hit belongs to your bank and that channel.
  • Failed transfers must return within two hours of the batch completing, with penal interest for delay beyond that.

Frequently asked questions

Is NEFT available on Sundays and bank holidays?

Yes, since 16 December 2019 batches settle on every day of the year. Your bank may still take its systems down for maintenance, which is a bank outage rather than a closure of NEFT.

What is the maximum I can send through NEFT?

The RBI imposes no ceiling. Any cap you meet comes from your bank, differs between mobile banking, net banking and the branch, and can often be raised on request.

Why has my NEFT not reached the payee yet?

Check which batch it fell into, because a transfer made just after a cut-off takes longer than one made just before. Past the two-hour window, ask your bank for the unique transaction reference and escalate.

Can I cancel a NEFT after confirming it?

Not unilaterally. Before the batch settles some banks attempt a stop through the branch, with no guarantee. After settlement, recovery depends on the beneficiary bank and the account holder agreeing to send it back.

Does the beneficiary name have to match the account number?

Credits are applied on the account number and IFSC, and a name mismatch is a common reason for a return. Some banks validate the name upfront and others do not, so check the account number twice.

Is NEFT safe for a large one-time payment?

It is safe, but where a payment must be final within minutes the gross settlement rail fits better, because each instruction settles individually. NEFT suits planned, dated payments.
