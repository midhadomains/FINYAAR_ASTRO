FINYAAR DICTIONARY · LOANS, CREDIT AND DEBT

Collateral

Collateral is an asset you pledge to a lender so that the loan is backed by something the lender can sell if you stop repaying. In India that is usually property, gold jewellery, a fixed deposit, a vehicle or listed securities, pledged through a mortgage, a lien, a hypothecation or a physical pledge. Because the lender holds a fallback, secured borrowing is priced below unsecured borrowing. No lender advances full value: the gap between the asset value and the loan is the margin.

Why security changes the price of a loan

A lender setting a rate is answering two questions: how likely is this borrower to stop paying, and how much money comes back afterwards. Your CIBIL score speaks to the first. Collateral speaks to the second, and it is the only one the lender can act on once things have gone wrong.

That gap explains the rate card. A home loan secured by the flat it funds sits at the cheap end of Indian retail credit, while a personal loan of the same size costs several percentage points more over a shorter tenure. The pledged asset is not fully yours to sell or pledge again until the charge is released.

Loan to value, margin and the haircut

One number governs every secured loan, and it compares what is lent against what the security is worth on the day of sanction.

The loan amount sanctioned is the money placed in your hands. The assessed value is the lender's own valuation, usually below the price you believe the asset would fetch. Multiplying by 100 states the result as a percentage, so ₹60,00,000 lent against a flat valued at ₹80,00,000 gives an LTV of 75 per cent.

Whatever is left over is the margin, your money standing in front of the lender's. Bankers state the idea in reverse and call it the haircut. Deposits attract thin haircuts, shares deep ones because prices gap down, and property haircuts reflect liquidity, since selling a flat takes months.

What Indian lenders take as security

The legal form of the charge decides how quickly a lender can reach the asset, and it varies sharply by asset class.

What actually happens if you default

A missed instalment does not put the asset on an auction block the following week. An account is generally classified as non-performing once dues run ninety days overdue, and enforcement starts only then. For mortgaged property, banks and notified NBFCs use the SARFAESI Act, 2002: section 13(2) requires a demand notice giving sixty days to clear the outstanding, and if that lapses, section 13(4) permits possession and sale without a court decree. Agricultural land sits outside the Act, and the borrower may appeal to the Debts Recovery Tribunal.

Movable security moves faster. Pledged gold is auctioned after notice, with any surplus returned. A sale that falls short still leaves you owing the shortfall, and the default reaches the credit bureaus, so the damage outlives the asset.

Worked example
Sunita Ghosh runs a catering business in Howrah and needs cash before the wedding season. She pledges 96 grams of 22-carat gold jewellery at an NBFC that values 22-carat gold at ₹6,250 per gram that day and applies a loan to value of 75 per cent. Six months later, with interest accrued and gold prices lower, where does she stand?

Step 1, what the security is worth. The valuation is 96 × ₹6,250, or ₹6,00,000. Only the metal counts. Stones, enamel and making charges are stripped out, which is why a jeweller's bill of ₹7,50,000 does not become a valuation of ₹7,50,000. Lower-carat pieces are converted to their 22-carat equivalent before the weight is totalled, so a 20-gram 18-carat chain contributes well under 20 grams to the calculation.

Step 2, what the lender advances. At 75 per cent the sanction is 0.75 × ₹6,00,000, so ₹4,50,000 reaches her account. The remaining ₹1,50,000 of value is the margin: still her wealth, but not borrowable.

Step 3, what the margin is quietly doing. Gold can fall about a quarter before the dues equal the metal value. The lender needs that room for a reason that catches many borrowers out. The collateral sits still while the loan grows, because interest accrues on it every month, so the ratio worsens even when gold does nothing at all.

Step 4, what a price fall triggers. Suppose gold drops 15 per cent, taking the valuation to ₹5,10,000, while the outstanding with accrued interest reaches ₹4,74,000.

Line itemAt sanctionAfter a 15% fall
Value of pledged gold₹6,00,000₹5,10,000
Amount outstanding₹4,50,000₹4,74,000
Loan to value75.0%92.9%
Margin in rupees₹1,50,000₹36,000
Cash needed to restore 75%Nil₹91,500

Step 5, the two ways back. To restore the ratio she must cut the loan to 0.75 × ₹5,10,000, which is ₹3,82,500. Paying in ₹91,500 does it, and so does pledging further gold worth roughly ₹1,22,000. If she does neither inside the notice period, the jewellery is auctioned and the surplus, if any, comes back to her.

So what? Margin is not the lender's generosity, it is the lender's cushion, and when it thins the demand for cash lands exactly when the asset is cheap and household finances are stretched. Borrow below the maximum permitted, keep the tenure short, and service the interest rather than letting it stack onto the principal. Sunita would have faced no call at all had she taken ₹3,60,000 instead of the full ₹4,50,000.

Check yourself
Q
Why will a bank lend a higher percentage against a ₹5,00,000 fixed deposit than against ₹5,00,000 of listed shares?
A: The haircut tracks how uncertain the recovery value is. A deposit has a fixed value and can be adjusted against the loan instantly, so almost no cushion is needed, while share prices can fall sharply before the lender manages to sell.

Key takeaways

  • Collateral gives the lender an enforceable claim on a named asset, which is why secured loans cost less.
  • Loan to value is the sanction divided by the lender's valuation; what is left over is your margin.
  • Haircuts track price volatility and how fast the asset can be sold.
  • Indian lenders accept property, gold, deposits, securities, vehicles and business stock.
  • Mortgaged property can be sold under SARFAESI after a sixty-day notice, and any shortfall stays your debt.

Frequently asked questions

Is collateral the same thing as a guarantor?

No. Collateral is an asset and the claim attaches to that asset, while a guarantor is a person whose general finances stand behind the loan.

Can I borrow if I have nothing to pledge?

Yes. Personal loans, credit cards and consumer durable finance are unsecured, so approval hinges on income proof and your bureau record. The rate is higher and the amount is capped by monthly income.

Who keeps the title deeds or the gold while the loan runs?

The lender does, and returns them on closure with a no-dues certificate. Insist on written acknowledgement of what was deposited, then follow up until the charge is removed from the records.

Can a lender demand more security mid-loan?

For assets whose price moves, yes, and the agreement will say so. Gold loans and loans against securities carry a top-up clause triggered when the LTV breaches its sanctioned level.

Can a bank sell my house without going to court?

Under SARFAESI a bank or notified NBFC can take possession and sell mortgaged property without a decree, once the account is non-performing and the sixty-day notice has expired unpaid. Agricultural land is exempt.

Does pledging a fixed deposit stop it earning interest?

No. A lien marks the deposit without breaking it, so it earns at the contracted rate for the full term, and you pay a spread over that rate on the borrowed amount.
