NPS (National Pension System)

NPS (National Pension System) is a voluntary, market-linked retirement scheme regulated by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA) that lets a saver build a retirement corpus across equity and debt assets. Returns are not guaranteed. At exit, the available lump sum, periodic-payout and annuity choices depend on the subscriber’s sector, corpus size, tenure and exit type.

What NPS is and how it works

NPS is a defined-contribution pension product. You open an account linked to a unique Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN), contribute over your working years, and a chosen pension fund manager invests the money. Because it is market-linked, the value moves with the underlying assets, unlike a government-set-rate product such as the PPF. The standard asset classes include equity (E), corporate debt (C), government securities (G), and alternative investments (A). Under Active Choice you set the mix within applicable limits; under Auto Choice the allocation changes with age. This exposure gives NPS long-run growth potential but also makes risk versus return important. The equity portfolio is diversified, although its mandate should not be assumed to match a particular index fund.

Tier 1 versus Tier 2 and how contributions grow

NPS has two main account types. Tier 1 is the retirement account with restricted withdrawals and pension-specific exit rules. Tier 2 is generally an optional, flexible investment account from which withdrawals can be made without the Tier 1 exit restrictions. It ordinarily has no additional contribution deduction. One exception is the Tier II Tax Saver Scheme available to eligible Central Government NPS subscribers, which has a three-year lock-in and Section 80C treatment. Contributions grow through compounding: investment returns remain in the account and can themselves earn returns.

Tax benefits

Under the old tax regime, an eligible Tier 1 personal contribution can provide an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B), over and above the combined ₹1.5 lakh ceiling that includes Section 80C. Personal deductions under Sections 80CCD(1) and 80CCD(1B) are generally unavailable under the new regime. Employer contributions under Section 80CCD(2) follow separate limits and can remain deductible under the new regime. The tax benefit lowers the effective contribution cost, but the corpus remains market-linked.

Current normal-exit and annuity rules

PFRDA amended the exit framework in December 2025 and again in July 2026. For the All Citizen and Corporate models, normal exit is now linked to completing the applicable vesting period or attaining age 60, as the rules provide. If accumulated pension wealth is above ₹12 lakh, up to 80% may generally be taken as lump sum or through approved payout methods, while at least 20% must fund an annuity. A subscriber may choose to annuitise more.

These regulatory withdrawal percentages are not the same thing as the income-tax exemption. Under the current tax framework, the exempt NPS lump-sum portion is generally limited to 60% of the corpus; pension received from an annuity is taxable as income. Taking a regulatory lump sum above the tax-exempt portion may have tax consequences. Government-sector, premature-exit, death and other cases use different tables, so confirm both PFRDA and income-tax rules at the time of exit.

Key takeaways

  • NPS is a PFRDA-regulated, market-linked retirement system; returns are not guaranteed.
  • Tier 1 follows retirement withdrawal and exit rules, while Tier 2 is generally flexible, with a special tax-saver exception for eligible Central Government subscribers.
  • Under the old regime, eligible personal Tier 1 contributions can receive an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B); employer contributions follow separate rules.
  • For All Citizen and Corporate normal exits above ₹12 lakh, the 2026 rules generally permit up to 80% lump sum and require at least 20% annuity.
  • Regulatory withdrawal permission and income-tax exemption are different: verify both when exiting.

Frequently asked questions

Is the NPS return guaranteed?

No. NPS is market-linked and invests across equity and debt assets. The account value changes with investment performance, and no future return is guaranteed.

How is NPS different from PPF?

PPF earns a government-set rate and follows its own maturity rules. NPS is market-linked and uses pension-specific exit rules that can require part of the corpus to fund an annuity.

Can I claim both Section 80C and the additional NPS deduction?

Under the old regime, an eligible personal NPS contribution of up to ₹50,000 can be claimed under Section 80CCD(1B) over and above the combined ₹1.5 lakh limit. Employer contributions under Section 80CCD(2) follow separate rules.

What is the difference between NPS Tier 1 and Tier 2?

Tier 1 is the retirement account and follows restricted withdrawal and exit rules. Tier 2 is generally flexible, although the special Tier II Tax Saver Scheme for eligible Central Government subscribers has a three-year lock-in.

How much must be used for an annuity at normal exit?

For All Citizen and Corporate normal exits above ₹12 lakh under the 2026 rules, at least 20% generally funds annuity and up to 80% may be taken through lump sum or approved payouts. Other sectors and corpus bands differ.

Can I exit NPS early?

Premature exit is allowed, but above the applicable small-corpus threshold it generally permits only up to 20% as lump sum and requires at least 80% for annuity. Confirm the rules for your sector and circumstances.
