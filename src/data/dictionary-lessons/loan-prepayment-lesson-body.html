Loan Prepayment

Loan Prepayment is paying back part or all of an outstanding loan ahead of the schedule written into your agreement. A part-prepayment is a lump sum that cuts the principal while the loan continues, and a full prepayment, usually called foreclosure, closes the account outright. Because Indian retail loans run on reducing-balance interest, every rupee prepaid stops attracting interest from the day it is credited. Under the RBI (Pre-payment Charges on Loans) Directions, 2025, floating-rate loans taken by individuals for non-business purposes carry no prepayment charge.

Part-prepayment and foreclosure are different animals

Part-prepayment leaves the loan alive. You transfer a lump sum, the lender applies it wholly to principal, and the account carries on with either a smaller instalment or a shorter remaining term. Bonuses, a matured fixed deposit and promotion arrears typically end up here.

Foreclosure ends the relationship. You pay the outstanding principal plus interest accrued to that date, and the lender must return the security and release the charge. For a home loan that means collecting original title deeds and having the mortgage entry removed from the land records. Ask for the no-dues certificate in writing, since the closure must also reach the bureaus and appear on your CIBIL report.

Reduce the EMI or shorten the tenure

Every part-prepayment forces one choice. Keep the EMI where it is and the loan finishes earlier; keep the tenure and the monthly outgo falls instead. The two are not equal in money terms, because holding the EMI steady keeps full repayment pressure on a shrinking principal.

Shortening the tenure is the cheaper route, often by a wide margin. Reducing the EMI still wins when the monthly outgo is straining the household budget, or a job change has made cash flow uncertain; breathing room has value an interest table cannot price. State your choice in the request itself, because a lender given no instruction applies its default, and that default usually lowers the EMI.

What a lender may charge you

The RBI (Pre-payment Charges on Loans) Directions, 2025 reset this area for loans sanctioned or renewed from 1 January 2026. A regulated entity may not levy a prepayment charge on a floating-rate loan given to an individual for a purpose other than business, whatever the source of the funds and with no minimum lock-in. Where the purpose is business and the borrower is an individual or a micro or small enterprise, larger lenders may not charge either.

SituationPrepayment charge
Floating rate, individual, non-business purposeNot permitted, whatever the source of funds
Floating rate, business purpose, individual or micro and small enterpriseNot permitted at larger lenders; smaller categories only above a ₹50 lakh sanctioned limit
Fixed rate loans, including many personal loansPermitted under the board-approved policy, only if disclosed

Nothing may be charged that was not disclosed in the sanction letter, the loan agreement and the Key Facts Statement. The RBI revises these rules, so check the current position before planning a large repayment.

Worked example
Rajat Kulkarni, a software engineer in Pune, took a ₹50,00,000 home loan for 20 years at an illustrative floating rate of 9 per cent, giving an EMI of ₹44,986. Three years in, an employee stock plan pays out and he wants to put ₹5,00,000 into the loan. Should he ask for a lower EMI or a shorter tenure?

Step 1, where the loan stands at year three. He has paid 36 instalments totalling ₹16,19,500, and the balance is still ₹46,91,900. Only about ₹3,08,100 of principal has been retired and roughly ₹13,11,400 went to interest. That lopsided split is the whole reason an early prepayment is so powerful: the balance the interest is charged on has barely moved.

Step 2, what the lump sum does on arrival. Prepayments are applied entirely to principal, with no part of them going to interest, so the balance drops straight to ₹41,91,900. Nothing else about the loan changes until he tells the bank which lever to pull, and the request form makes him choose.

Step 3, hold the EMI and cut the tenure. Keeping the instalment at ₹44,986, the loan now runs about 161 more months instead of 204. He finishes roughly three years and seven months early, and those are the most expensive months at the front of the schedule, not the cheap ones at the back.

Step 4, hold the tenure and cut the EMI. Keeping 204 months, the instalment falls to about ₹40,193, freeing ₹4,793 every month. Useful for cash flow, but the loan still runs its full course.

The same ₹5,00,000, paid on the same day, is worth three times as much under one instruction as under the other, and the gap of ₹9,73,200 is pure interest that either does or does not get charged. Since this is a floating-rate home loan to an individual for a non-business purpose, no prepayment charge applies, so nothing eats into the saving.

So what? Prepay early and keep the EMI untouched if the household can carry it, because the instruction you give the bank matters as much as the amount you send. Take the lower EMI only when the monthly figure is the problem you are actually trying to solve.

Check yourself
Q
Two borrowers prepay ₹2,00,000 on identical loans. One asks for a shorter tenure, the other for a lower EMI. Who saves more interest, and why?
A: The one who shortens the tenure. Holding the EMI steady keeps the full monthly payment attacking a smaller principal, so fewer months of interest are ever charged.

Key takeaways

  • Part-prepayment cuts the principal and keeps the loan running; foreclosure clears it and releases the security.
  • Prepayments go wholly to principal, so they cancel every month of interest that principal would have carried.
  • Holding the EMI and shortening the tenure saves far more than lowering the EMI.
  • The earlier you prepay, the larger the saving, because the balance is highest then.
  • Floating-rate loans to individuals for non-business purposes carry no prepayment charge.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a limit on how much I can prepay in a year?

For floating-rate retail loans covered by the Directions there is no lock-in and no restriction on the source of funds. Lenders may still set a minimum amount per prepayment.

Should I prepay the home loan or invest the money instead?

Compare the loan rate with the return you realistically expect after tax elsewhere. Prepaying delivers a certain, risk-free saving equal to the loan rate, a high bar for any investment.

Does prepaying a loan hurt my credit score?

No. Closing a loan on your own terms is recorded in good standing and generally helps, since it lowers your outstanding debt.

Which loan should I clear first if I have several?

Attack the highest rate first, which almost always means credit card dues, then personal loans, then secured loans. Rate matters more than balance size.

Can a lender refuse to accept my prepayment?

Not for the categories named in the Directions, where the right to prepay without charge is explicit. For other loans your agreement governs, so read the prepayment clause.

What paperwork should I collect after foreclosure?

Ask for a no-dues certificate, all original documents and a letter releasing the charge. Verify later that the account shows as closed on your credit report.
