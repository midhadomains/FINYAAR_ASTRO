TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) is a mechanism under the Income Tax Act, 1961 by which the person making certain payments — salary, interest, rent, professional fees and more — deducts a small slice of tax before handing over the money, and deposits it with the government against the recipient’s PAN. In short, tax is collected as income arises rather than only at year end.

What TDS is and why the government uses it

When you receive income in India, the payer is often legally required to hold back a portion as tax and pay it directly to the Central Government on your behalf. The payer is called the deductor, you are the deductee, and the amount held back is TDS. Every deduction is stamped against your PAN, so it flows into your tax account automatically.

The government uses TDS for three reasons. First, it collects revenue steadily through the year instead of waiting for one lump sum. Second, it widens the tax net, because deduction happens at the source of income and is hard to hide. Third, it reduces evasion — the person paying you reports what they paid, creating a paper trail. For you as the deductee, TDS is not an extra tax. It is an advance instalment of your own tax liability that you later reconcile when filing your Income Tax Return (ITR).

How the deduction is calculated

Each type of payment sits under its own section of the Act, with its own rate and threshold. The deductor applies the prescribed rate to the payment amount once the threshold is crossed.

For salary under Section 192, there is no flat rate. The employer estimates your annual income, applies the slab rates of your chosen regime, and spreads the resulting tax evenly across twelve months. For most other payments a fixed percentage applies once a limit is breached. A crucial rule: if you do not give a valid PAN, TDS may be deducted at a much higher rate (commonly 20 percent), so always share your PAN.

Interest from a bank fixed deposit is one of the most common examples. Under Section 194A the bank generally deducts 10 percent once your interest for the year crosses the applicable threshold. Submitting Form 15G or 15H, if you satisfy the relevant conditions, can stop this deduction.

Form 26AS, AIS, and adjusting against final tax

Every rupee of TDS deducted against your PAN appears in your Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement (AIS) on the income tax portal. These are your consolidated tax passbooks. When you file your ITR, you total your income, compute tax on it, and then subtract the eligible TDS credit already paid. If TDS exceeds your final liability, the excess is refunded to your bank account after the return is processed. If it falls short, you pay the balance as self-assessment tax. The deductor also issues a TDS certificate: Form 16 for salary and Form 16A for non-salary payments — keep these, because they document the deduction if the credit is ever disputed.

Worked example
Ananya Rao, a graphic designer in Pune, earns a salary and also holds a bank fixed deposit. In the financial year her employer deducts salary TDS, and her bank deducts TDS on FD interest of ₹60,000. Her total tax liability for the year, computed on all income, turns out lower than the total TDS already deducted. How does the reconciliation work, and does she get anything back?

Step 1 — Identify each deduction. Ananya’s employer deducts ₹48,000 as salary TDS under Section 192 across the year. Her bank sees FD interest of ₹60,000, which crosses the indicative ₹50,000 threshold, so under Section 194A it deducts 10 percent.

Step 2 — Compute the FD TDS. Applying the formula, ₹60,000 × 10% = ₹6,000. Because Ananya gave her PAN, the higher rate for not furnishing PAN does not apply.

Step 3 — Add up total TDS credited. Her Form 26AS and AIS show both entries against her PAN.

Step 4 — Compare with final liability. When Ananya files her ITR, she totals her salary and interest income, claims her eligible deductions, and arrives at a final tax of ₹41,000. Her total TDS of ₹54,000 is higher.

Step 5 — Claim the refund. Excess = ₹54,000 − ₹41,000 = ₹13,000. She reports both TDS entries in her return, and after the return is processed the Income Tax Department refunds ₹13,000 to her bank account.

So what? TDS is not lost money when the corresponding credit is correctly reported and claimed in your return. The ₹6,000 the bank deducted was not an extra charge — it simply pre-paid part of Ananya’s tax. Because her overall liability was lower, the surplus came back to her. The lesson: always check that Form 26AS and AIS match your Form 16 and 16A before filing, so every eligible rupee deducted is claimed.

Check yourself
Your bank deducted TDS on your FD interest, but you forgot to report it in your ITR. Is that money simply lost?
A: It is not automatically lost, but you must claim the eligible credit. TDS should appear in Form 26AS and AIS; report it in your return so it can be set off against your tax or refunded, subject to the applicable filing and rectification rules.

Key takeaways

  • TDS is advance tax collected at the point income is paid, deposited against your PAN by the payer (deductor).
  • Salary TDS uses estimated slab rates under Section 192; bank interest commonly uses a 10 percent rate under Section 194A once the applicable threshold is crossed.
  • Not furnishing a valid PAN can trigger a higher deduction rate, commonly 20 percent.
  • Form 26AS and AIS list reported deductions; Form 16 (salary) and Form 16A (non-salary) are the corresponding certificates.
  • Excess eligible TDS can be refunded after you file your ITR, so reconciliation and filing are essential.

Frequently asked questions

Is TDS an extra tax on top of income tax?

No. TDS is a part-payment of the very income tax you owe. It is deducted early and adjusted against your final liability when you file your return, so it never adds to your total tax.

The bank deducted TDS on my FD but my income is below the taxable limit — what now?

You may be able to submit Form 15G, or Form 15H if you are a senior citizen, to the bank at the start of the year to stop deduction if you meet the applicable conditions. If TDS was already deducted, file your ITR and claim the eligible amount back as a refund.

Where do I check how much TDS has been deducted against my PAN?

Log in to the income tax e-filing portal and view your Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS). Both consolidate TDS entries reported against your PAN across employers, banks and other deductors.

What is the difference between Form 16 and Form 16A?

Form 16 is the TDS certificate for salary, issued by your employer under Section 192. Form 16A covers non-salary TDS — interest, rent, professional fees and similar payments — and is issued by the respective deductor. Both document tax paid on your behalf.

Why was TDS deducted at 20 percent on my interest?

This can happen when a valid PAN was not provided or accepted for the account. Deduction without PAN can attract a higher rate, commonly 20 percent for interest. Update your PAN with the deductor and reconcile the credit through your ITR.

If too much TDS was deducted, how do I get the excess back?

File your Income Tax Return, report all your income and verify the TDS shown in Form 26AS and AIS. If the tax you owe is less than the eligible TDS credit, the difference is refunded to your bank account after the return is processed.
