FINYAAR DICTIONARY · MUTUAL FUNDS AND SIP

ELSS

ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme) is an equity mutual fund carrying a statutory three-year lock-in that qualifies for a deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. SEBI requires the category to hold at least 80 per cent in equity, so the money goes into listed Indian companies and nothing guaranteed. Three years is the shortest lock-in among the 80C options, though the deduction reaches only a taxpayer filing under the old regime. The tax break is certain the day you claim it; the return is certain on no day at all.

Eighty per cent in shares, by rule

Every rupee entering an ELSS lands in a portfolio that SEBI scheme categorisation requires to keep at least four-fifths in equity and equity-related instruments. Within that floor the manager is free, so two funds wearing the same label can hold almost nothing in common. The tax status attaches to the wrapper, never to the stock picking inside it.

An ELSS therefore behaves exactly like the equity mutual fund it is: its NAV drops through a bad quarter and the deduction cushions none of that fall. Saving on a tax bill and taking equity risk are two separate transactions travelling in one product.

The deduction belongs to the old regime

Section 80C lets a taxpayer reduce gross total income by the amount invested, up to a ceiling the section fixes and that every other 80C claim of the year draws from as well. Since the regime under Section 115BAC became the default filing position, one point deserves stating plainly: a new-regime filer receives no 80C deduction at all, so the tax argument for an ELSS disappears. Run both computations on your own income first, and read old versus new tax regime beside this page.

The lock-in is indifferent to that choice. Units stay locked for three years from allotment because the scheme is built that way, not because a deduction was claimed.

Each instalment starts its own clock

Confusion arrives when the money goes in as a SIP. Each instalment buys a parcel of units at that day's NAV and serves its own three years from its own allotment date, so the scheme opens one instalment at a time.

D is the date you want to redeem on, t the allotment date of one instalment, A with subscript t its rupee amount, and NAV with subscript t the value at which it was allotted, so the fraction is the units that instalment bought. The summation gathers only parcels allotted at least three years before D; everything younger contributes zero. Stopping the SIP releases nothing early.

Once units come free nothing forces a sale. Gains on redemption are capital gains on an equity-oriented scheme, and since the lock-in already ran three years the long-term treatment applies, with the annual exemption the Act allows. Confirm current rates and thresholds with the Income Tax Department.

Worked example
Ritwik Bhattacharya, a software tester in Kolkata, has put ₹8,000 into an ELSS on the seventh of every month since 7 May 2022. On 15 June 2025 his sister needs ₹2,00,000 for a hospital deposit. Three years have passed since he began, so he assumes the fund is open. How much can he actually withdraw?

Step 1, what has gone in. Counting 7 May 2022 through 7 June 2025 gives thirty-eight instalments, so ₹3,04,000 has left his bank account. His statement on 15 June 2025 shows a market value well above that figure. Neither number answers the question he is asking.

Step 2, which parcels have served their time. Only units allotted on or before 15 June 2022 have completed three full years by that date. Two instalments qualify, those of 7 May 2022 and 7 June 2022. The July 2022 instalment unlocks on 7 July 2025, three weeks after his sister needs the money, and every parcel after it queues up behind that one.

Step 3, turning free units into rupees. The NAV on 15 June 2025 is ₹92.40.

Step 4, the cheque he can write. Redeeming 261.333 units at ₹92.40 raises ₹24,147. Those units cost him ₹16,000, so the gain of ₹8,147 is long term, the holding period running well past twelve months, and taxed accordingly.

Step 5, the shortfall. He wanted ₹2,00,000 and the scheme releases a little over a tenth of it. The rest of his ₹3,04,000 sits on the statement, growing, and out of reach until each instalment date rolls around. Waiting until 7 July 2025 adds only one more parcel of 125 units.

So what? A three-year lock-in on a SIP is thirty-six separate three-year lock-ins wearing one name. An ELSS earns its place as a long-horizon equity holding that happens to reduce a tax bill under the old regime, and it fails badly as a store of money you might need at short notice. Ritwik's emergency money belonged somewhere he could reach on a Sunday.

Check yourself
Q
You start an ELSS SIP in January 2026 and stop it after twelve instalments. On what date is the entire holding free to redeem?
A: December 2029, three years after the December 2026 instalment. Stopping a SIP releases nothing already bought, and each parcel counts from its own allotment date.

Key takeaways

  • An ELSS is an equity fund first, required to hold at least 80 per cent in equity and equity-related instruments.
  • The Section 80C deduction exists only under the old regime, so a new-regime filer gets the lock-in and no benefit.
  • Three years is the shortest 80C lock-in, paid for with direct market exposure.
  • On a SIP, every instalment locks separately for three years from its own allotment date.
  • Returns are market linked and unguaranteed, so ELSS money should not be money you may need soon.

Frequently asked questions

Can I withdraw from an ELSS before three years in an emergency?

No. The lock-in is statutory rather than a fee-based restriction, so the fund house cannot release units early for a job loss or a medical bill. That is the sharpest difference between an ELSS and an ordinary equity fund carrying only an exit load.

Is ELSS worth it if I file under the new tax regime?

Only as an equity fund. You accept a three-year lock-in and receive no deduction, while an ordinary scheme with a similar mandate offers the same exposure freely.

Which is better for tax saving, ELSS or PPF?

They answer different questions. PPF pays a notified rate and locks money for fifteen years, while ELSS offers equity returns and equity volatility over three. Many households hold both.

How is the gain taxed when I finally redeem?

As long-term capital gains on an equity-oriented scheme, since the units necessarily crossed twelve months during the lock-in, with an annual exemption before tax. Confirm the current rate and threshold with the Income Tax Department.

Does the IDCW option change the lock-in?

Units remain locked for three years under either option. What changes is that an IDCW payout distributes cash during the lock-in, taxable in your hands, while growth leaves everything inside the scheme.

What happens to ELSS units if the investor dies during the lock-in?

Units generally become transmissible to the nominee or legal heir after a specified period from allotment, once the fund house completes its process. The scheme information document sets out the treatment.
