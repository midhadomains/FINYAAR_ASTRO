Compound Interest

the interest you earn on both your original money and on the interest already added to it, so your balance grows faster over time.

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on your original amount (the principal) and on the interest that has already been added to it. In plain words, you earn interest on your interest. This snowball effect is why a small, steady investment can grow into a large sum over many years.

It is the opposite of simple interest, where you earn interest only on the original principal and never on the interest built up along the way. Over short periods the difference is tiny. Over 15 to 20 years it becomes the single biggest driver of how much wealth you end up with.

How compounding works

Every compounding period (a year, a quarter, a month) the interest earned is added back to your balance. The next period's interest is then calculated on this new, larger balance. The more frequently interest is compounded, the faster the balance grows, because interest starts earning its own interest sooner.

Worked example

Suppose you invest ₹1,00,000 at 10% per year, compounded annually, for 10 years.

Solution. A = 1,00,000 × (1.10)10 = 1,00,000 × 2.5937 = ₹2,59,374. With simple interest you would earn ₹10,000 a year, or ₹1,00,000 in total, giving ₹2,00,000. Compounding added an extra ₹59,374 purely because interest kept earning interest.
Check yourself
Using the Rule of 72, how long does money take to double at 12% a year?
About 6 years. The Rule of 72 estimates the doubling time as 72 divided by the rate, so 72 / 12 = 6 years.

Key takeaways

  • You earn returns on your returns, not just on the money you put in.
  • Time is the most powerful ingredient: starting early beats investing more later.
  • More frequent compounding (monthly vs yearly) grows money slightly faster.
  • The same force works against you on loans and credit cards, where interest compounds on what you owe.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal, so it grows in a straight line. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus all previously earned interest, so it grows faster and faster over time.

Is compound interest good or bad?

It is excellent when you are investing or saving, because your money grows faster. It works against you on borrowings such as credit card debt, where the amount you owe compounds if you do not pay in full.

How can I calculate compound interest quickly?

Use the Rule of 72: divide 72 by the annual return to estimate the years it takes your money to double. At 12% a year, money doubles in roughly 72 / 12 = 6 years.
