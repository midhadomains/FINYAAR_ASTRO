FINYAAR DICTIONARY · BANKING SAVINGS AND DEPOSITS

Savings Account

Savings Account is a deposit account held with a bank or post office that keeps your money safe, lets you withdraw it on demand, and pays interest on the daily closing balance. The savings account interest rate is set by each bank and typically ranges from about 2.5 to 7 percent a year, so it varies by bank and balance rather than being fixed for all time. Deposits are insured up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, which makes it one of the safest places to keep money you may need at short notice.

What a savings account is

A savings account is the most common bank account an Indian household holds. You deposit money you may need at short notice, and the bank keeps it liquid, meaning you can withdraw it any time through a branch, an ATM, a card, or UPI. In exchange for holding your balance, the bank pays a modest rate of interest. Unlike a fixed deposit, there is no lock-in and no maturity date, so the balance stays fully accessible. This makes it the natural home for an emergency fund and for money moving in and out during the month.

Interest and how it is calculated

Since 2010 the Reserve Bank of India has required banks to compute savings interest on the daily closing balance. The bank looks at the balance at the end of each day, applies the annual rate for that one day, and adds up the daily amounts over the quarter. Interest is then credited quarterly to your account. The rate is decided by each bank and can change, so do not treat a headline rate as permanent.

Here Bd is the closing balance on day d, r is the annual rate as a decimal, and n is the number of days in the period. Because each day is counted separately, a large balance parked for even a few days earns its share. This is the same daily-accrual idea behind compound interest, though the credit happens only four times a year.

Types and features

Banks offer several variants: a regular savings account, a zero-balance Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account under financial inclusion rules, salary accounts, senior citizen accounts, and accounts for minors or women with tailored perks. Most regular accounts require a minimum average balance, and falling below it attracts a penalty. Features usually bundled in include a debit card, cheque book, net and mobile banking, and free UPI transfers. If you want to earn more on idle cash, a recurring deposit or fixed deposit pays a higher rate in return for locking the money away.

Safety and tax

Deposits in a savings account are insured by the DICGC, a subsidiary of the RBI, up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, covering principal and interest together. Interest earned is fully taxable as income from other sources. However, individuals below 60 can claim a deduction of up to ₹10,000 a year on savings interest under Section 80TTA, while senior citizens get up to ₹50,000 under Section 80TTB. Worked example
Ananya keeps her salary account at a bank paying 3.5 percent a year on the daily closing balance. During the month of June (30 days) her balance moves as her salary arrives and her bills go out. On 1 June she starts with ₹40,000. Her salary of ₹60,000 credits on 3 June, taking the balance to ₹1,00,000. On 10 June she pays rent and card dues totalling ₹70,000, leaving ₹30,000. On 20 June she pays a ₹10,000 SIP, leaving ₹20,000, which she holds till month end. How much interest does June earn?

Step 1 — break June into balance blocks. Interest is on the daily closing balance, so group the days by the balance that applied on each day and count how many days each balance stayed.

10–19 Jun1030,0003,00,000
20–30 Jun1120,0002,20,000
Total30—13,00,000

Step 2 — sum the daily balances. Adding the last column gives ₹13,00,000 as the sum of closing balances across all 30 days. This single figure captures the whole month because each day is weighted by its own balance.

Step 3 — apply the daily rate. The annual rate is 3.5 percent, so r = 0.035. The daily factor is r divided by 365. Interest for June equals the sum of daily balances times that factor: 13,00,000 × 0.035 ÷ 365 = about ₹124.66.

Step 4 — place it in the quarter. This ₹124.66 is not paid out on 30 June. It accrues and joins the interest for April, May and June, and the bank credits the combined quarterly figure at the end of the quarter. Ananya sees one lump sum land in her account, not three monthly entries.

So what? Notice that the ₹60,000 salary earned full interest only for the seven days it actually sat in the account before the big outflow. Timing matters: money held longer earns more. Over a year the small savings rate still means idle lakhs earn little, which is why a fixed deposit or liquid fund suits money you will not touch for months.

Check yourself
Q
If your savings account pays 4 percent a year and your closing balance stays exactly ₹50,000 every day for a 91-day quarter, roughly how much interest is credited?
A: 50,000 × 0.04 × 91 ÷ 365 ≈ ₹498. The bank adds this at the end of the quarter.

Key takeaways

  • A savings account is a fully liquid deposit account that pays interest and lets you withdraw on demand.
  • Interest is computed on the daily closing balance and credited quarterly; the rate is set by each bank and can change.
  • Deposits are insured by the DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank.
  • Interest is taxable, but Section 80TTA gives up to ₹10,000 relief (under 60) and 80TTB up to ₹50,000 for seniors.
  • For money you will not need soon, a fixed or recurring deposit pays more than a savings account.

Frequently asked questions

What is the current savings account interest rate in India?

It varies by bank and balance, commonly between 2.5 and 7 percent a year. Each bank sets its own rate and may change it, so confirm the latest figure with your bank or the RBI.

How often is savings interest paid?

Interest accrues daily on the closing balance and is credited to your account once every quarter.

Is my money in a savings account safe?

Yes, deposits are insured by the DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, covering principal and interest together.

Do I pay tax on savings account interest?

Yes, it is taxable as income. You may deduct up to ₹10,000 under Section 80TTA if below 60, or up to ₹50,000 under Section 80TTB if a senior citizen.

What is a minimum average balance?

It is the average balance a regular account must maintain over a month or quarter. Falling below it usually attracts a penalty, though zero-balance accounts avoid this.

Should I keep all my savings here?

Keep an emergency buffer and monthly spending in it, but move surplus you will not need soon to a fixed deposit, recurring deposit, or investments that earn more.
