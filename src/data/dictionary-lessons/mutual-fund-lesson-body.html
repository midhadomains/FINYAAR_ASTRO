FINYAAR DICTIONARY · MUTUAL FUNDS AND SIP

Mutual Fund

an investment vehicle that pools money from many investors and uses it to buy a professionally managed portfolio of stocks, bonds, or other assets.

What is a mutual fund?

A mutual fund collects money from thousands of investors and invests it as one large pool in a mix of stocks, bonds, or other securities. A professional fund manager, working for an Asset Management Company (AMC), decides what to buy and sell. When you invest, you receive units that represent your share of the whole portfolio.

This lets a small investor own a slice of a large, diversified portfolio for as little as a few hundred rupees, something that would be expensive and time-consuming to build alone. In India, mutual funds are regulated by SEBI, which sets rules to protect investors.

How does a mutual fund work?

The value of one unit is called the Net Asset Value (NAV), calculated at the end of each trading day. If the fund's holdings rise in value, the NAV rises and so does your investment. You can invest either as a one-time lump sum or through a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) that puts in a fixed amount every month.

Funds charge a small annual fee called the expense ratio to cover management and running costs. It is deducted automatically from the fund, so a lower expense ratio leaves more of the return with you.

Main types of mutual funds

Equity funds invest mainly in stocks and aim for higher long-term growth with higher risk. Debt funds invest in bonds and are steadier but usually lower-returning. Hybrid funds mix both. Index funds simply copy an index such as the Nifty 50 at very low cost. Your choice depends on your goal, time horizon, and how much ups and downs you can tolerate.

Example

Worked example

You invest ₹10,000 in an equity fund when its NAV is ₹50.

How many units do you get, and what are they worth if the NAV later rises to ₹60?
Solution. Units = 10,000 / 50 = 200 units. At a NAV of ₹60 they are worth 200 × 60 = ₹12,000, a gain of 20% before costs and taxes.

Key takeaways

  • You get instant diversification and professional management for a small amount.
  • NAV reflects the per-unit value of the fund and updates daily.
  • The expense ratio quietly reduces your returns, so lower is generally better.
  • Returns are not guaranteed; equity funds in particular can fall in the short term.

Frequently asked questions

Are mutual funds safe?

Mutual funds are regulated by SEBI and your money is held separately from the AMC, so the fund house cannot run away with it. However, the value of your units rises and falls with the market, so returns are not guaranteed and you can lose money, especially over short periods.

What is NAV in a mutual fund?

NAV, or Net Asset Value, is the price of one unit of the fund. It equals the total value of the fund's holdings minus its expenses, divided by the number of units outstanding, and is published at the end of each trading day.

Is a SIP the same as a mutual fund?

No. A mutual fund is the investment product. A SIP is simply a method of investing in it, putting in a fixed amount at regular intervals instead of a single lump sum.
