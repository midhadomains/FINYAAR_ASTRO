FINYAAR DICTIONARY · MUTUAL FUNDS AND SIP

STP

STP (Systematic Transfer Plan) is a fund house arrangement that shifts a fixed amount from one of its own schemes into another at a regular interval, usually out of a liquid or debt scheme and into an equity scheme. Indians receiving a bonus, a retirement payout or property sale proceeds use it to enter equity in slices rather than all at once. Both legs sit inside one asset management company, which is the practical limit on what an STP can do. Each transfer is a redemption from the source, so it is a taxable event and can attract an exit load.

Where a lump sum waits its turn

A large sum poses an awkward choice. A savings account earns very little while the decision is postponed; committing everything to equity on one morning exposes the lot to whatever that morning turns out to have been. An STP splits the difference, parking the sum in a low-volatility scheme that keeps earning and feeding it across on a schedule.

The instruction names an amount and a frequency, offered as daily, weekly, fortnightly or monthly. Some AMCs run a capital appreciation variant moving only the gains, leaving the original sum in the source mutual fund. Most investors use the fixed-amount version, which behaves like a SIP funded from a fund.

A source whose NAV itself swings defeats the purpose, since money still waiting would carry the risk being phased in. Hence the short, dull end of the debt spectrum.

Every transfer is a sale, with everything that follows

The word transfer does the damage. The fund house redeems units of the source scheme and buys units of the target, and the Income Tax Act treats that redemption exactly as a withdrawal to your bank. Gains on the source units are capital gains in the year of transfer, whether or not a rupee reached you.

For a debt-oriented source, gains on units acquired in recent years are generally added to total income and taxed at slab without indexation, under the rules for specified mutual funds; an arbitrage source counts as equity-oriented instead. These provisions have been amended more than once, so confirm what applies to your acquisition date with the Income Tax Department. Exit load is the second consequence, since liquid funds generally apply a graded load on days one to six, with no load from day seven under the published schedule. Check the source scheme's current terms.

What the averaging is really worth

A fixed rupee amount buys more units when the target NAV is low and fewer when it is high, so average cost is a harmonic mean.

P with a bar is the average cost of one unit, A with subscript t the amount transferred on date t, P with subscript t the target NAV that date, and the denominator the total units bought. For equal transfer amounts, the result never exceeds the plain average of the NAVs. If transfer amounts differ, the comparison must use an amount-weighted arithmetic average instead.

That gain is small. The real work an STP does is behavioural and belongs to asset allocation: it stops an investor committing a life-changing sum on one arbitrary date. In a rising market the staggered route finishes behind a lump sum.

Worked example
Devendra Rathi of Jaipur receives ₹12,00,000 from the sale of a plot. He buys 400 units of a liquid fund at a NAV of ₹3,000 and sets up a monthly STP of ₹2,00,000 into a flexi cap fund. Where does he stand after six transfers, and what does he owe on the source scheme?

Step 1, the source scheme at work. The illustrative liquid fund NAV grows by about 0.55 per cent a month while the money waits, lifting its NAV from ₹3,000 to ₹3,100.37 over six months. Step 3, his average cost. He moved ₹12,00,000 into the flexi cap scheme and received 8,047.203 units, an average of ₹149.12 each. The plain average of the six NAVs is ₹149.35, so the averaging effect saved 23 paise a unit, about ₹1,850 altogether. Useful, and nowhere near the main point.

Step 4, the tax bill he did not expect. The 392.412 units redeemed from the liquid fund cost ₹3,000 each, or ₹11,77,236, and fetched ₹12,00,000. That realised gain of ₹22,764 is taxable this financial year, spread over six redemptions he never thought of as sales. A further ₹23,526 remains in the liquid fund, ₹762 of it unrealised. No exit load arose, monthly transfers sitting well outside the seven-day window.

Step 5, the road not taken. Buying the flexi cap outright at ₹145.00 on day one, ₹12,00,000 would have bought 8,275.862 units, worth ₹13,11,310 at the closing NAV of ₹158.45. The STP route leaves ₹12,75,079 in equity plus ₹23,526 in the liquid fund, or ₹12,98,605. The lump sum finished ₹12,705 ahead.

So what? Devendra paid roughly ₹12,705 of forgone gain plus a small tax bill to spread his entry across six dates. This reduces dependence on one entry price but does not protect the invested money from market losses. Had the market fallen through those six months, the ledger would read the other way. An STP buys protection against regret rather than extra return, and the six redemptions it generates belong in your tax return whether or not you noticed them.

Check yourself
Q
Your friend says an STP is tax free because the money never leaves the fund house. Where is the error?
A: Each transfer redeems units of the source scheme, and a redemption is taxable wherever the proceeds go. The gain is assessed in the year of transfer even though no money reached a bank account.

Key takeaways

  • An STP moves a fixed amount at fixed intervals between two schemes of one fund house, usually debt into equity.
  • The source stays short and stable so waiting money does not carry the risk being phased in.
  • Every transfer is a redemption of source units, taxable that year and able to attract an exit load.
  • Averaging lowers cost per unit only modestly; the benefit is avoiding a badly timed single entry.
  • In a rising market a lump sum beats an STP, the cost of insuring against the opposite case.

Frequently asked questions

Can I run an STP between two different fund houses?

No. An STP runs only between schemes of one asset management company. Across fund houses you would redeem to your bank and run a SIP into the other.

Is an STP better than investing the lump sum at once?

It depends on what the market does next, which nobody knows. Lump-sum entry can finish ahead when the target rises faster than the source while transfers are pending. An STP spreads entry dates but guarantees neither better returns nor protection from losses.

How long should an STP run?

There is no universal duration; choose a schedule that fits your intended allocation, time horizon and tolerance for entry-date risk. Stretching it further leaves a large share in a low-return source, working against the goal that prompted the equity allocation.

Does the receiving scheme have its own lock-in or load?

Each purchase in the target starts its own holding period and exit-load clock, so redeeming soon after the STP ends can attract a load on recent transfers.

What is the difference between an STP and an SWP?

An SWP sends money out to your bank; an STP keeps it inside by buying units of another scheme. Both redeem units, so either can realise capital gains or losses.

Can I stop or change an STP midway?

Yes, through the fund house or your platform, usually with a few working days of notice. Investors often stop one early and move the balance after a fall.
