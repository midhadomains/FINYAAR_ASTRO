FINYAAR DICTIONARY · RATIOS AND VALUATION

P/E Ratio

a valuation ratio that compares a company's share price to its earnings per share, showing how much investors pay for each rupee of profit.

What is the P/E ratio?

The Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio is the most widely used valuation measure in the stock market. It answers a simple question: how much are investors willing to pay for one rupee of a company's annual profit? A P/E of 20 means investors pay ₹20 for every ₹1 of earnings.

It is calculated by dividing the current share price by the earnings per share (EPS). A higher P/E usually signals that the market expects strong future growth, while a lower P/E can signal modest expectations or that a stock is out of favour.

How to interpret P/E

A high P/E is not automatically expensive, and a low P/E is not automatically cheap. A fast-growing company can deserve a high P/E because its future profits are expected to rise sharply. A low P/E may reflect a struggling business rather than a bargain. The ratio is most useful compared against the company's own history and against peers in the same industry, since normal P/E levels vary widely by sector.

Trailing vs forward P/E

Trailing P/E uses actual earnings from the past 12 months, so it is factual but backward-looking. Forward P/E uses forecast earnings for the next 12 months, so it reflects expectations but depends on estimates that may be wrong. Looking at both gives a fuller picture.

Example

Worked example

A stock trades at ₹500 and its earnings per share over the last year were ₹25.

What is its P/E, and what does it tell you?
Solution. P/E = 500 / 25 = 20. Investors pay ₹20 for every ₹1 of annual profit. If a competitor with similar growth trades at a P/E of 12, the first stock looks relatively expensive, and you would want to understand why.

Key takeaways

  • P/E shows how much investors pay per rupee of earnings.
  • High P/E often means high growth expectations, not simply overvaluation.
  • Only compare P/E within the same industry and against the stock's own history.
  • It fails for loss-making firms, where EPS is negative and P/E is meaningless.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good P/E ratio?

There is no single good number. It depends on the industry and growth rate. A stable utility might normally trade at a P/E of 12 to 18, while a fast-growing tech firm might trade at 40 or more. Always compare against peers and the company's own past levels.

Is a low P/E ratio always better?

Not necessarily. A low P/E can mean a stock is undervalued, but it can also mean the market expects earnings to fall or that the business faces problems. It is a starting point for research, not a buy signal on its own.

What does a negative P/E mean?

A negative P/E arises when a company has negative earnings, meaning it is making a loss. In that case the ratio is not meaningful, and analysts turn to other measures such as price-to-sales or EV/EBITDA.
