FINYAAR DICTIONARY · MUTUAL FUNDS AND SIP

ETF

an Exchange-Traded Fund: a basket of securities, often tracking an index, that you buy and sell on the stock exchange like a single share.

What is an ETF?

An Exchange-Traded Fund (ETF) is a basket of securities, most often designed to track an index like the Nifty 50 or a gold price, that trades on the stock exchange just like a share. Buy one unit and you own a small slice of the whole basket, giving instant diversification.

Like an index fund, most ETFs are passive and low cost. The key difference is how you buy them: an ETF trades on the exchange throughout the day at a live market price, whereas a mutual fund is bought and sold once a day at its NAV.

How ETFs work

You need a demat and trading account to buy an ETF, and you pay the market price, which stays very close to the fund's underlying value (its NAV) thanks to market-makers. ETFs cover indices, gold, bonds, and specific sectors. Because they are passive and traded directly, expense ratios are usually very low.

ETF vs index fund

Both track an index cheaply. An ETF trades intraday and needs a demat account, and its price can differ slightly from NAV. An index fund is a regular mutual fund bought at end-of-day NAV, with no demat account needed and easy SIPs. Pick the ETF for intraday flexibility, the index fund for simple, automated investing.

Key takeaways

  • An ETF is a basket of securities that trades on the exchange like a share.
  • Most ETFs passively track an index at very low cost.
  • You need a demat account, and you trade at live market prices.
  • ETFs suit intraday flexibility; index funds suit simple SIP investing.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an ETF and a mutual fund?

A mutual fund is bought and sold once a day at its NAV, directly from the fund house. An ETF trades on the exchange all day at a live market price and needs a demat account. Both can track the same index.

Do I need a demat account to buy an ETF?

Yes. Because ETFs trade on the stock exchange, you need a demat and trading account to buy and hold them, unlike a regular mutual fund.

Are ETFs cheaper than mutual funds?

ETFs are usually low cost because most are passive and traded directly, but remember to factor in brokerage and the small gap between price and NAV when you trade.
