Personal Loan

Personal Loan is the standard Indian name for an unsecured, fixed-tenure borrowing that a bank or NBFC advances against your income and repayment record rather than against any asset. Nothing is pledged, so the lender has no property to fall back on and prices that exposure into a rate well above a secured loan. Repayment runs by equated monthly instalment over a term usually between one and five years, with a processing fee taken out of the disbursal. The end use is unrestricted, which is why lenders scrutinise the borrower rather than the purpose.

What a lender is pricing when nothing is pledged

Every loan is priced off expected loss, which has two halves: the chance you stop paying, and how much can be recovered if you do. A home loan answers the second half easily, because the flat is collateral and can be sold. A personal loan has no answer. If repayment stops the lender holds a civil claim and a long recovery process, so the rate must carry the whole loss.

That is why a personal loan costs several percentage points more than a housing or gold loan to the same borrower, and why the amount sanctioned tracks your income so closely. With no asset to lean on, the lender's only security is your monthly cash flow.

How eligibility and your rate get decided

Your CIBIL score is the first filter and the biggest lever on the rate offered, since it summarises how you have handled earlier borrowing. Below a cut-off the application is declined; comfortably above it, the same lender may shave a margin off the advertised rate.

Income comes next, tested as a ratio rather than an absolute. Lenders cap the share of net monthly income that all your instalments may consume, so an existing car loan or a heavy credit card balance shrinks what you can borrow. Employment quality matters too: a salaried applicant with two years of service is read differently from a self-employed applicant assessed on tax returns. Age, banking relationship and tenure feed the final quote.

The instalment formula, and what stretching the tenure does

The EMI is a level payment covering interest on the outstanding balance plus a slice of principal, with the interest share falling every month.

P is the amount sanctioned, n the number of monthly instalments, and r the monthly rate, being the annual rate divided by twelve as a decimal, so 14 percent a year makes r equal to 0.011667. A longer tenure lowers the instalment but keeps the balance alive, so the interest bill climbs steeply.

Fees, foreclosure and what the rate leaves out

A processing fee, commonly a low single-digit percentage of the sanction plus GST, is deducted before the money reaches you. That matters more than its size suggests: interest runs on the full sanction while you receive less than it, so the effective annual cost sits above the rate on the sanction letter. Every charge must appear in the Key Facts Statement, and one never disclosed there cannot be recovered.

On closing early, the rules changed for loans sanctioned or renewed from 1 January 2026. RBI directions bar prepayment charges on floating rate loans taken by individuals for non-business purposes, whatever the source of funds and with no lock-in. Most personal loans are fixed rate, where a foreclosure charge may still apply within the disclosed schedule, so check your agreement before planning a loan prepayment.

Worked example
Kavita Rao works at a logistics firm in Nagpur and needs ₹4,00,000 for her daughter's admission and hostel deposit. Her bank sanctions a three-year personal loan at an illustrative 15.5 percent a year with a processing fee of 2 percent plus GST. What does the loan actually cost her, and how does it compare with the rate she was quoted?

Step 1, what it costs to take the money. The processing fee is 2 percent of ₹4,00,000, or ₹8,000, and GST at 18 percent adds ₹1,440. The bank deducts ₹9,440 and credits her ₹3,90,560, but the loan on its books is ₹4,00,000 and interest runs on that figure from day one.

Step 2, the instalment. The monthly rate is 15.5 divided by 12, or 0.012917. With P of ₹4,00,000 and n of 36, the formula gives an EMI of about ₹13,967. Over three years she pays 36 such instalments, a total of ₹5,02,812.

Step 3, the interest bill. Subtracting the ₹4,00,000 she borrowed leaves ₹1,02,812 of interest. Adding the upfront ₹9,440 takes the full cost of the loan to ₹1,12,252, which is roughly 28 paise of cost for every rupee that actually reached her account.

Step 4, where the money goes month by month. Her first instalment carries about ₹5,167 of interest and only ₹8,800 of principal, because interest is charged on the whole outstanding balance. By the final year the proportions have flipped, which is why prepaying in the first half of the tenure saves far more than prepaying near the end.

Step 5, the rate she is really paying. Money in hand was ₹3,90,560, not ₹4,00,000, while the instalment was calculated on the larger figure. Solving for the rate that equates ₹3,90,560 today with 36 payments of ₹13,967 gives roughly 17.2 percent a year, close to two percentage points above the headline.

So what? The advertised rate is a starting point, not the price. Kavita should compare offers on the effective annual cost after fees and on the foreclosure terms in the Key Facts Statement, and she should take the shortest tenure her monthly budget can carry, because every extra year adds interest far faster than it lightens the instalment.

Check yourself
Q
Why does a personal loan carry a higher rate than a home loan to the same borrower?
A: A home loan is secured on the property, so a default can be met partly by selling it. A personal loan has no such security, so the lender recovers its entire expected loss through the rate.

Key takeaways

  • A personal loan is unsecured, so the rate carries the lender's whole expected loss and sits above secured borrowing.
  • Your credit score, income, existing instalments and job stability decide the amount sanctioned and the rate quoted.
  • Stretching the tenure cuts the EMI but raises total interest sharply, as the balance stays outstanding longer.
  • The processing fee comes out of the disbursal, so the effective annual cost exceeds the sanction letter rate.
  • Every charge must appear in the Key Facts Statement, and an undisclosed one cannot be recovered from you.

Frequently asked questions

What credit score do I need for a personal loan?

There is no common cut-off; each lender sets its own. A score high in the range gets the sharpest pricing, a middling score an approval at a higher rate, and a poor score a decline.

Can I prepay a personal loan without a penalty?

For loans sanctioned or renewed from 1 January 2026 on a floating rate to an individual for a non-business purpose, prepayment charges are barred. Fixed rate personal loans, the common form, may still carry a foreclosure charge under the agreement.

Does a personal loan hurt my credit score?

Applying triggers a hard enquiry, and several applications in a short span read badly. The loan raises your outstanding debt, which weighs on the score at first, while a clean run of on-time EMIs lifts it over the tenure.

Is a top-up on a home loan cheaper than a personal loan?

Usually yes, because the top-up is secured on the same property. The trade-off is that it stretches the debt over the remaining home loan tenure, so a small borrowing accrues interest for a very long time.

Can I claim tax benefit on a personal loan?

There is no deduction for a personal loan as such. Where the money is demonstrably used for a purpose the Income Tax Act recognises, such as house construction or a business, the interest may be claimable, so keep records.

What happens if I miss an EMI?

You are charged late payment and bounce fees, and the miss is reported to the credit bureaus, where it stays on record for years. Repeated defaults invite recovery action and make future borrowing far harder.
