FINYAAR DICTIONARY · MUTUAL FUNDS AND SIP

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)

a way of investing a fixed amount into a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually every month, instead of putting in a single lump sum.

What is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a disciplined way to invest in mutual funds. You choose a fixed amount, say ₹5,000, and it is automatically debited from your bank account every month and used to buy fund units. It turns investing into a habit and removes the pressure of timing the market.

Because you invest the same rupee amount each month, you automatically buy more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high. This is called rupee cost averaging, and it smooths out the effect of market ups and downs over time.

Why SIPs work so well

SIPs combine two powerful forces: rupee cost averaging, which lowers your average cost per unit, and compounding, where returns earned start generating their own returns. The longer you stay invested, the more the compounding effect dominates, which is why SIPs reward patience far more than clever timing.

Example

Worked example

You start a ₹5,000 monthly SIP. Month 1 NAV ₹100, month 2 dips to ₹80, month 3 recovers to ₹125.

What is your average cost per unit after three months?
Solution. Units bought: 50 + 62.5 + 40 = 152.5 for ₹15,000 invested. Average cost = 15,000 / 152.5 = about ₹98.4 per unit, even though the NAV averaged ₹101.7. The dip actually helped you accumulate more units cheaply.

Does a SIP protect you from losing money?
No. A SIP invests in mutual funds, whose value moves with the market, so it can still fall in the short term. What it does is spread your buying over time, lowering the risk of investing everything at a market peak.

Key takeaways

  • Invest a fixed amount regularly, regardless of market level.
  • Rupee cost averaging means you buy more when markets are down.
  • It builds discipline and removes the stress of timing the market.
  • The real magic shows up over 7 to 10 years and longer, thanks to compounding.

Frequently asked questions

Can I lose money in a SIP?

Yes. A SIP invests in mutual funds, whose value moves with the market, so your investment can fall, especially in the short term. Over long periods, staying invested through the ups and downs has historically smoothed out much of this risk, though past performance does not guarantee future returns.

Can I stop or pause a SIP?

Yes. SIPs are flexible. You can pause, increase, decrease, or stop them at any time without penalty, and you keep the units already bought. Stopping is different from redeeming, which is when you actually sell your units.

Is SIP better than a lump sum?

Neither is always better. A SIP suits regular income earners and volatile markets because it spreads risk over time. A lump sum can do better when markets rise steadily after you invest. Many investors use both.
