EBITDA

a measure of a company's operating profitability, calculated before subtracting interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation.

What is EBITDA?

EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation. It strips out four items from profit to show how much a company earns from its core operations, before the effects of how it is financed (interest), where it operates (taxes), and non-cash accounting charges (depreciation and amortisation).

The goal is to compare the underlying operating performance of different companies on a like-for-like basis. Because it removes financing and accounting differences, EBITDA is widely used to compare firms with different debt levels or tax situations, and as a rough proxy for operating cash flow.

How to calculate EBITDA

There are two common routes to the same number. Start from net profit and add back the four items, or start from operating profit (EBIT) and add back depreciation and amortisation.

Equivalently, EBITDA = Operating Profit (EBIT) + Depreciation + Amortisation. Both give the same figure.

What EBITDA does not tell you

EBITDA is popular but has real blind spots. It ignores the cost of maintaining and replacing assets (capital expenditure), it ignores changes in working capital, and it flatters companies that carry a lot of debt by hiding interest costs. A business can show healthy EBITDA yet still struggle to generate actual cash. Always read it alongside cash flow and net profit, never on its own.

Example

Worked example

A company reports: Net Profit ₹40 cr, Interest ₹10 cr, Taxes ₹15 cr, Depreciation ₹20 cr, Amortisation ₹5 cr.

What is its EBITDA?
Solution. EBITDA = 40 + 10 + 15 + 20 + 5 = ₹90 crore. This shows the operating earnings power before financing and accounting effects, which is why analysts use it to compare this firm against a rival with very different debt or tax.

Key takeaways

  • EBITDA shows core operating profit before interest, taxes, and non-cash charges.
  • It helps compare companies with different debt and tax profiles.
  • It is a rough proxy for operating cash flow, not the same thing.
  • It ignores capital spending and working capital, so it can overstate financial health.

Frequently asked questions

Why do companies use EBITDA?

Because it isolates operating performance from financing and accounting choices, making it easier to compare businesses and to value them. It is also used in loan agreements and in valuation multiples such as EV/EBITDA.

Is a higher EBITDA always better?

Generally a higher EBITDA signals stronger operating earnings, but not always. A company can boost EBITDA while burning cash on capital expenditure or drowning in debt. Judge it alongside cash flow, capex, and net profit.

What is the difference between EBIT and EBITDA?

EBIT is Earnings Before Interest and Taxes, and it still subtracts depreciation and amortisation. EBITDA adds those two non-cash charges back, so EBITDA is always equal to or larger than EBIT.
