Monetary Policy

Monetary Policy is the set of decisions through which a central bank influences the price and availability of money in an economy. In India it is framed by the Reserve Bank of India under a flexible inflation targeting mandate in the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, with the policy rate set by a six-member Monetary Policy Committee. The Central Government, in consultation with the Reserve Bank, fixes the consumer price inflation target once in five years, currently 4 percent within a band of 2 to 6 percent. Its instruments run from the repo rate to reserve requirements and open market operations.

What a central bank is actually adjusting

Money has a price, and that price is the interest rate. By changing what banks pay to borrow overnight and earn to park spare funds, the Reserve Bank shifts the cost of every loan and the reward on every deposit downstream. Cheaper money pulls households into borrowing and firms into investing, lifting demand and, if supply lags, prices. Dearer money reverses it.

India pins that discretion to a number. Under Section 45ZA the Central Government, with the Reserve Bank, sets a consumer price inflation target every five years, aiming at price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth. Average inflation outside the band for three consecutive quarters is a failure to maintain that target.

Six votes decide the policy rate

Section 45ZB constitutes the Monetary Policy Committee and charges it with setting the rate needed to hit that target. Three members come from the Reserve Bank: the Governor as chairperson, the Deputy Governor for monetary policy, and an officer nominated by the Central Board. Three external experts appointed by the Central Government for four-year terms make up the rest. Four form a quorum, each has one vote, and the Governor breaks a tie.

The law requires at least four meetings a year; in practice there are six. The resolution appears the day a meeting ends, the minutes on the fourteenth day after, recording how each member voted.

A corridor, a midpoint, and the levers beside it

Overnight money is kept inside a band, with the repo rate at its midpoint. Since a bank can always borrow at the ceiling or park at the floor, the call money rate rarely strays outside it.

How a decision in Mumbai reaches a household budget

Transmission begins with banks, whose overnight funding cost moves the moment the corridor shifts. Since 1 October 2019 new floating rate retail loans, the home loan and vehicle categories included, must be linked to an external benchmark, and most banks chose the repo rate itself.

R is the rate charged, B the external benchmark adopted, and s the spread the bank adds for operating cost, margin and credit profile. The benchmark must reset at least once in three months, so a change in B reaches the borrower within a quarter while s holds. The EMI follows from the annuity relation.

P is the outstanding principal, r the monthly rate, meaning R divided by twelve as a decimal, and n the instalments left. Deposits follow with a lag and no mandate, so borrowers feel a cut before holders of a fixed deposit do.

Worked example
Devendra Rathod teaches at a college in Nagpur. He owes ₹40,00,000 on a repo-linked floating rate home loan with 216 instalments left, and keeps ₹5,00,000 in a maturing bank deposit. Suppose the Monetary Policy Committee cuts the policy rate by 50 basis points, his loan rate resets from 8.75 percent to 8.25 percent, and his bank drops its deposit rate from 7.00 percent to 6.50 percent. What happens to his money over the next year?

Step 1, the loan before the cut. A rate of 8.75 percent divided by twelve is 0.72917 percent a month. Putting ₹40,00,000, that rate and 216 instalments into the annuity relation gives an EMI of ₹36,836, an annual outgo of ₹4,42,032.

Step 2, the loan after the reset. At 8.25 percent the monthly rate falls to 0.6875 percent. The same principal over the same 216 instalments now needs an EMI of ₹35,606, an annual outgo of ₹4,27,272. He pays ₹1,230 less each month and ₹14,760 less over the year, if the bank passes the cut through as a lower instalment rather than a shorter tenure.

Step 3, the deposit side of the ledger. On renewal, ₹5,00,000 at 7.00 percent would have earned ₹35,000 a year. At 6.50 percent it earns ₹32,500, leaving him ₹2,500 worse off as a saver, and that loss usually lands later than the loan benefit.

Step 4, the household in aggregate. Netting the two, Devendra is better off by ₹14,760 minus ₹2,500, or ₹12,260 over twelve months. His debtor side is eight times his saver side, so a symmetric cut lands asymmetrically. Had he held the EMI at ₹36,836 and let the tenure shrink instead, the loan would close in roughly 200 months rather than 216.

So what? A committee decision reported as one line of news moved ₹12,260 through a single Nagpur household in a year, and would have moved it the other way had the rate risen. Whether a policy change helps or hurts you turns on which side of your balance sheet the money sits, so read a resolution against your own loans and deposits, not the headline.

Check yourself
Q
Why does a repo rate cut reach a borrower faster than a depositor?
A: Floating rate retail loans must be linked to an external benchmark and reset at least once in three months, so the pass-through is mandated. Deposit rates carry no such requirement and move at the bank's own discretion.

Key takeaways

  • India follows flexible inflation targeting, the price target set every five years.
  • A six-member Monetary Policy Committee sets the policy rate, meets at least four times a year, and publishes minutes on day fourteen.
  • The corridor runs from the standing deposit facility at the floor to the marginal standing facility at the ceiling.
  • It also uses the cash reserve ratio, the statutory liquidity ratio and open market operations.
  • Transmission runs from the policy rate through lending rates to EMIs, slower to deposits, at once to bonds.

Frequently asked questions

Who decides the repo rate in India?

The Monetary Policy Committee, by majority vote. Three of its six members come from the Reserve Bank and three are external experts appointed by the Central Government. The Governor chairs and breaks a tie.

What is the current repo rate and inflation reading?

Both change frequently, so no figure printed on a page stays accurate. Read the latest Monetary Policy Committee resolution on the RBI website, and the Ministry of Statistics release for the price index.

How does monetary policy differ from fiscal policy?

Monetary policy is run by the central bank through interest rates and liquidity; fiscal policy by the government through the Union Budget. Both shape demand and prices from different institutions.

What does a hawkish or dovish stance mean?

Hawkish means leaning towards tighter policy, weighting inflation control even at some cost to growth; dovish is the opposite. The committee also announces a formal stance, such as neutral.

How does monetary policy affect the stock market?

Lower rates cut what companies pay to borrow and the rate at which future profits are discounted, both of which support valuations. Banking, housing and vehicle stocks react most.

What happens if inflation stays outside the tolerance band?

Average inflation breaching either tolerance level for three consecutive quarters is a failure to maintain the target. The Reserve Bank must then report to the Central Government with the reasons and the remedial actions proposed.
