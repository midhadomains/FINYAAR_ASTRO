FINYAAR DICTIONARY · BANKING SAVINGS AND DEPOSITS

Fixed Deposit

Fixed Deposit is a banking product in which you lock a lump sum with a bank or NBFC for a fixed tenure at a pre-agreed interest rate, and receive your principal plus interest at maturity. It is one of India's most trusted low-risk savings instruments; eligible bank deposits are protected up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank by DICGC insurance.

What is a Fixed Deposit?

A Fixed Deposit (FD), also called a term deposit, is a deposit where you hand a bank a single amount for a chosen period, ranging from 7 days to 10 years, and the bank pays you a fixed rate of interest that is decided on the day you open the FD. Unlike a savings account, the rate does not change during the term, so you know your maturity value in advance. This certainty is exactly why FDs are a default choice for many Indian households.

Imagine Priya, a Pune schoolteacher, receives a Diwali bonus of ₹5,00,000. She does not want to risk it in a mutual fund right now, so she books a 5-year FD at 7.1% per annum. She now knows the exact rupee amount she will get back, which helps her plan without worrying about market swings. That predictability is the core appeal of an FD, and the trade-off is captured in the idea of risk vs return: lower risk usually means lower long-run return.

How the interest is calculated

For short deposits, banks may use simple interest instead, where interest is not added back to the base. A quick sanity check on doubling time comes from the Rule of 72: at 7.1%, your money roughly doubles in 72 ÷ 7.1 ≈ 10 years.

An FD is not the same as a Recurring Deposit: an FD takes one lump sum upfront, while an RD collects a fixed amount every month, much like the discipline of a SIP in a mutual fund.

Worked example
Priya invests ₹5,00,000 in a cumulative Fixed Deposit at 7.1% per annum, compounded quarterly, for 5 years. What will she receive at maturity, and how much better is this than a plain simple-interest FD?

Step 1 — Set up the numbers. Here P = ₹5,00,000, r = 0.071, n = 4 (quarterly), and t = 5 years. The quarterly rate is r/n = 0.071 ÷ 4 = 0.01775 (1.775% per quarter), applied over n × t = 20 quarters.

Step 2 — Apply the formula.

The growth factor (1.01775) raised to the 20th power is 1.421746. So A = 5,00,000 × 1.421746 = ₹7,10,873 (rounded to the nearest rupee).

Step 3 — Find the interest. Interest = A − P = ₹7,10,873 − ₹5,00,000 = ₹2,10,873. Here is how the balance grows year by year:

Step 4 — Compare with simple interest. A simple-interest FD would pay P × r × t = 5,00,000 × 0.071 × 5 = ₹1,77,500, for a maturity of ₹6,77,500. Quarterly compounding earns ₹2,10,873 − ₹1,77,500 = ₹33,373 extra for the same deposit.

So what? Notice the interest rises each year (₹36,457 in year 1 up to ₹48,308 in year 5) because compounding puts earlier interest to work. The lesson is practical: if regular income is not needed, a cumulative FD lets interest compound, and you should compare the maturity value rather than only the headline rate. Priya should also remember that her real gain is lower after inflation.

Tax, safety and premature withdrawal

Tax: FD interest is generally taxable as income. Banks may deduct TDS once interest crosses the applicable annual threshold; eligible depositors can submit Form 15G/15H where the legal conditions are met. A 5-year tax-saver FD has a lock-in and may qualify for a Section 80C deduction under the applicable tax regime and current rules.

Safety: Eligible bank deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, with principal and interest counted together. NBFC and company deposits are not covered by DICGC merely because they are called FDs.

Premature withdrawal: Breaking an FD early usually means receiving the rate applicable to the actual period held, often with an additional penalty. A healthy CIBIL score is not needed to open an FD, and many banks allow loans or overdrafts against an FD, subject to their terms.

Check yourself
A person books a ₹2,00,000 FD at 8% p.a. compounded quarterly for 1 year. Roughly what is the maturity amount?
Quarterly rate = 8% ÷ 4 = 2% over 4 quarters: A = 2,00,000 × (1.02)4 = 2,00,000 × 1.08243 ≈ ₹2,16,486, or about ₹16,486 interest — slightly more than the ₹16,000 simple interest would produce.

Key takeaways

  • A Fixed Deposit locks a lump sum for a set tenure at a rate fixed on day one, giving a known maturity value.
  • Maturity uses compound interest: A = P(1 + r/n)^(n×t), often compounded quarterly for cumulative bank FDs.
  • Cumulative FDs compound interest; non-cumulative FDs pay regular income.
  • Eligible bank deposits are DICGC-insured up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank.
  • Interest is generally taxable, while tax-saver FD benefits depend on the applicable tax regime and current rules.
  • After inflation and tax, real returns can be modest, so FDs are commonly used for safety and short-to-medium-term goals.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Fixed Deposit completely safe?

Bank FDs are among the lower-risk options in India because the rate is fixed and eligible deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, including principal and interest. Amounts above that limit remain exposed to the bank, and NBFC or company FDs do not receive the same DICGC cover.

Can I withdraw my FD before maturity?

Most regular FDs allow premature withdrawal, but the bank usually applies the rate for the actual period held and may levy a penalty. A 5-year tax-saver FD normally cannot be broken early. A loan or overdraft against the FD may be an alternative, subject to the bank's terms.

How is FD interest taxed?

FD interest is generally added to your taxable income and taxed at the applicable slab rate. TDS rules and thresholds can change, and TDS is not the final tax liability. Eligible taxpayers may use Form 15G or 15H when the statutory conditions are satisfied.

FD or mutual fund — which is better?

They serve different goals. An eligible bank FD offers a fixed return and limited deposit insurance, making it useful for capital stability and shorter goals. A mutual fund may offer higher long-term return potential but carries market risk and no guaranteed return. The suitable choice depends on time horizon, liquidity needs, and risk capacity.

What is the minimum and maximum I can invest?

Minimum deposit amounts and product limits vary by institution. Many banks accept relatively small opening amounts, while large deposits may be classified differently. DICGC insurance remains capped at ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank across eligible deposits in the same right and capacity.

Do senior citizens get a higher FD rate?

Many banks offer resident senior citizens an additional interest rate over the regular card rate, but the premium varies by bank and tenure. Tax deductions and TDS treatment also depend on current law and the depositor's circumstances.
